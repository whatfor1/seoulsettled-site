Lost a job in Korea and heard you might be owed money from two different directions? You are, and they're not the same pot. Severance pay (퇴직금) comes from your employer, for time served, regardless of why you left — I cover that separately in my [severance pay guide](/korea-severance-pay-foreign-workers/). Job-seeker's benefit (구직급여, usually called 실업급여) is a completely different thing: it's paid by the government's Employment Insurance fund while you're actively looking for your next job, and whether you get it at all depends on your visa type, why you left, and how long you were enrolled — none of which affects severance.

This guide covers 구직급여 specifically: who's actually enrolled in Employment Insurance as a foreign worker, the 180-day qualifying rule, how much you get per day, how many days you get it for, and the 12-month clock that cuts off anything you haven't claimed yet. Every number below is checked against Korea's plain-language legal portal (easylaw.go.kr, run by the Ministry of Government Legislation) and the Ministry of Employment and Labor's own pages, current as of November 3, 2026.

## First: are you even enrolled?

This is the step that trips up foreign workers specifically, and it depends entirely on your visa.

**Mandatory enrollment.** If you hold F-2 (residence), F-5 (permanent residency), or F-6 (marriage migrant) status, you're a mandatory Employment Insurance subscriber — same as a Korean national. Your employer reports your enrollment from your hire date, and if they didn't, it can be applied retroactively.

**Voluntary enrollment, employer consent required.** Most work-visa holders — E-1 through E-7, E-9, E-10, C-4, F-4, and H-2 — fall under 임의가입 (voluntary coverage). You are not automatically in the system. You have to ask your employer to enroll you, and the employer has to agree, before you're ever a subscriber at all. If you never had that conversation, you were likely never enrolled, and there's no 구직급여 to claim no matter how long you worked.

Why this matters in practice: if you're on an E-9 or H-2 visa, don't assume the deductions on your payslip include unemployment insurance. Check your 4대보험 enrollment status directly — the National Pension Service's integrated portal or a call to your employer's payroll will confirm it — before you plan around a benefit you may never have been signed up for.

## The 180-day rule

Assuming you were enrolled, [easylaw.go.kr's summary of qualifying conditions](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMain.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=514&ccfNo=7&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=2) — citing Employment Insurance Act Article 40 — sets out what you need, and all four have to be true at once:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Your combined insured work days add up to 180 days or more within the 18 months before you left the job</li>
  <li>You have the will and ability to work, but haven't found a new job yet</li>
  <li>Your reason for leaving isn't one of the disqualifying reasons under the Employment Insurance Act</li>
  <li>You're making active efforts to find reemployment</li>
</ul>

The 180-day count is cumulative, not continuous — short stints at different employers can add up to 180 days as long as they're within that 18-month window and each one had you enrolled.

## Were you fired, laid off, or did you quit? It changes everything

If your employer ended the contract, or your fixed-term contract simply expired without renewal, you generally qualify. If you voluntarily resigned, the default answer is no — but there's a recognized list of exceptions under the Employment Insurance Act's enforcement rule (고용보험법 시행규칙 별표 2) that still counts as a qualifying separation even though you initiated it. Easylaw.go.kr confirms this exceptions list exists and includes situations like workplace discrimination, sexual harassment the employer failed to address, company bankruptcy or a mass layoff, and reaching a contract's end date or retirement age — but the full table is long and fact-specific, so don't self-diagnose from a summary. Bring your resignation letter and the circumstances to your local employment center (고용센터) and ask directly whether your situation fits one of the listed exceptions before assuming you're excluded.

## How much you get, per day

The daily benefit (구직급여일액) is calculated from your 기초일액 — your average daily wage, generally based on your last 3 months of pay before leaving:

- **Standard rate:** 60% of your 기초일액.
- **If your 기초일액 is low enough to hit the statutory floor:** the benefit is instead calculated as 80% of the "minimum basic daily amount" — your hourly minimum wage at the time you left, multiplied by 8 hours.

Both calculations are capped. Per the [Ministry of Employment and Labor's own announcement](https://www.moel.go.kr/news/enews/report/enewsView.do?news_seq=18736) of its December 2025 Cabinet-approved amendment, the 2026 daily ceiling on 구직급여일액 is **68,100 won**, up from 66,000 won — the ministry raised it specifically because the 2026 minimum wage increase (to 10,320 won per hour) was about to push the statutory floor above the old ceiling, which isn't allowed. The same announcement raises the wage-daily-amount cap feeding into this calculation from 110,000 won to 113,500 won. Working the floor formula through with the confirmed 2026 numbers — 10,320 won × 8 hours × 80% — puts the 2026 daily floor at **66,048 won**. I'm showing that as a calculation rather than a quoted figure because the ministry's own release confirms the 68,100 won ceiling by name but doesn't restate the floor number directly; the 66,048 won result follows from the official minimum-wage and formula sources, not from a secondary site's rounding.

Multiply either daily figure by roughly 30 and you're looking at a monthly range of roughly 1,980,000 to 2,040,000 won for someone at the floor or ceiling — most people land somewhere between the two, based on their actual pay.

## How many days you're paid for

The number of days you can draw this benefit (소정급여일수) depends on your age when you left the job and how long you were insured, per [easylaw.go.kr's benefit-days table](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMain.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=722&ccfNo=2&cciNo=3&cnpClsNo=1):

| Insured period | Under 50 | 50+ or disabled |
|---|---|---|
| Under 1 year | 120 days | 120 days |
| 1–3 years | 150 days | 180 days |
| 3–5 years | 180 days | 210 days |
| 5–10 years | 210 days | 240 days |
| 10+ years | 240 days | 270 days |

There's also a 7-day waiting period (대기기간) that starts from the day you report as unemployed — no benefit is paid for those 7 days. The one exception: day laborers in construction skip the waiting period entirely and get paid from their report date.

## The clock you can't miss: 12 months

This is the detail that catches people who assume they can claim later. Your benefit period (수급기간) runs for **12 months from the day after you left your job** — not 12 months from when you apply. [Easylaw.go.kr's Q&A on benefit-period extensions](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/OnhunqueansInfoRetrieve.laf?onhunqnaAstSeq=82&onhunqueSeq=3864) states it plainly: "수급기간 12개월이 초과되면 소정급여일수가 남아 있더라도 남은 구직급여를 받을 수 없습니다" — if that 12-month window closes, any unused days from your 소정급여일수 table above are gone, even if you never claimed them. The only extension is for people who genuinely can't work during that window for pregnancy, childbirth, childcare, or illness or injury — that can push the window out by up to 4 years, but it has to be requested, not assumed.

## How to actually apply

The process runs in a fixed order, per easylaw.go.kr:

1. **Register as a job-seeker** on [고용24 (work24.go.kr)](https://www.work24.go.kr) — 채용정보 → 구직신청. You need this done before the next step.
2. **File a 수급자격 인정신청서** (benefit-eligibility recognition application) at your local employment center. You can file at the center near your residence, your workplace, or wherever is most convenient to reach.
3. **Attend 실업인정** — periodic in-person or online check-ins at the employment center confirming you're still job-searching. Your 수급자격증 (benefit eligibility certificate) is issued at your first 실업인정 date, once your eligibility is confirmed.

## When it goes wrong

**You're not sure if you were ever enrolled.** Don't guess — ask your former employer's payroll department for your 고용보험 가입 이력, or check directly with the National Pension Service's 4대보험 portal. If you were on a voluntary-enrollment visa and nobody ever filed the paperwork, there's no benefit to claim, and no retroactive fix after the fact.

**You resigned and think you don't qualify, but the circumstances feel unfair.** Don't self-reject. Bring your documentation — resignation letter, any written harassment complaints, wage records if unpaid wages were the trigger — to your local 고용센터 and ask them to check it against the Employment Insurance Act's exceptions table directly. The specific list is detailed enough that it's genuinely their call, not a guess from any guide.

**You're close to the 12-month deadline and still have unused days.** File now. There is no late exception for the 12-month benefit period itself outside the documented medical/childcare deferral — once it closes, the remaining days are gone regardless of why you waited.

**Your application gets rejected for a missing document or a disputed separation reason.** Ask the caseworker exactly what's missing or disputed before resubmitting. A document gap is usually same-visit fixable; a disputed separation reason (for example, your employer's paperwork says "voluntary resignation" and you disagree) is worth raising directly with the center, since the determination is based on the actual circumstances, not just what one party wrote down.

**You're on an E-9 or H-2 visa and your contract just ended.** Whether you were enrolled for 구직급여 is a separate question from the deadlines Korea's work-visa system puts on your next step — those immigration-side deadlines for finding a new workplace and getting the transfer approved are real and tight, but I couldn't confirm the exact day counts on an official page for this guide, so don't take a number from a search result. Call the Employment Permit System's own foreign worker support line or your regional 고용센터 directly and get the current figure for your specific situation before you make any plan around it.

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Confirmed whether your visa requires mandatory or voluntary Employment Insurance enrollment</li>
  <li>Confirmed with payroll or the NPS portal that you were actually enrolled, not just eligible to be</li>
  <li>Added up insured days across all recent jobs — need 180+ within the last 18 months</li>
  <li>Checked whether your separation reason qualifies, especially if you resigned</li>
  <li>Registered as a job-seeker on work24.go.kr before filing anything else</li>
  <li>Filed the 수급자격 인정신청서 at a 고용센터</li>
  <li>Marked the 12-month deadline from the day after you left — not from today</li>
</ul>
