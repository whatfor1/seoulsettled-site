If you finished a degree at a Korean university and moved to D-10 (job-seeking) status to look for work, the clock you're on got longer in 2025. The Ministry of Justice extended the maximum time international graduates can hold D-10 status from **2 years to 3 years**, effective **October 29, 2025**, and changed how often you have to go back and renew it. That's real, and it's confirmed directly from the Ministry's own announcement — but it's also narrower than a lot of secondary write-ups make it sound, and the status still has hard stops that catch people who assume "3 years" means unlimited room to keep looking.

## What actually changed in October 2025

The reform is specifically about international students who complete a degree at a Korean university and transition from D-2 (student) to D-10 to search for work — confirmed on the Ministry of Justice's own announcement page, [법무부 공식 게시물](https://www.moj.go.kr/bbs/moj/189/601118/artclView.do) (게시일 2025.12.02, covering a change that took effect 2025.10.29):

<ul class="checklist">
  <li><strong>Maximum cumulative D-10 stay:</strong> raised from 2 years to 3 years</li>
  <li><strong>Renewal unit:</strong> changed from 6 months per renewal to 1 year per renewal</li>
  <li><strong>Internship activity while job-seeking:</strong> the old total cap of 1 year across all internships was removed entirely; the limit per single company was raised from 6 months to 1 year</li>
</ul>

If you're on D-10 through a different route — direct overseas recruitment, a conversion from E-1 through E-7 status, or anything other than a Korean-university degree track — don't assume this specific 3-year figure applies to your case the same way. D-10 covers more than one entry path, and this announcement names graduates of domestic degree programs specifically. Confirm your own category's current limit on [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr) or by calling **1345** before you plan around a number from this article.

## Who D-10 is for, and what it doesn't let you do

D-10 exists to let you search for work in positions that correspond to E-1 through E-7 status (professor through specific-activities/specialist roles), or to prepare a technology startup, without needing an employer lined up first. Eligibility runs through a points-based screening Immigration calls the 점수제 (point system) — factors reportedly include education level, Korean-language ability, and age, among others. I'm deliberately not publishing a specific pass-score number here: the figures circulating on visa-consultancy blogs weren't traceable back to a current Ministry of Justice notice I could verify directly, and a cutoff that changes by notice is exactly the kind of number that goes stale fastest. Check your own score and the current pass line on HiKorea or at your jurisdictional immigration office before you apply.

What D-10 does **not** give you is general work rights. It's a preparation status — in principle, profit-making activity is restricted, same as any other non-work visa category. Two things change that:

- **Approved internships** tied to your job search, now subject to the loosened caps above instead of the old 1-year total limit.
- **A separate part-time work permit** (체류자격외활동허가) if you want paid side work beyond an internship — the same permission freelancers on other visas file for. [Korea's civil-service listing for this permit](https://www.gov.kr/portal/service/serviceInfo/PTR000050540) sets the fee at **120,000 won**, non-refundable once accepted for processing.

## Renewing D-10 status

Renewal follows the standard extension process under [Article 25 of the Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1000869901&lsId=001707&chrClsCd=010202&print=print) — file before your current permitted stay ends, through HiKorea. The fee is the general extension rate: **60,000 KRW**, with a **20%** discount if you file the e-application online instead of in person. Since October 2025, each renewal covers 1 year instead of 6 months for graduates under the reform above, which in practice means fewer trips to the immigration office over the same 3-year span, not just a longer total runway.

## Moving from D-10 to an actual work visa

Landing a job doesn't convert your status automatically. You still need a separate 체류자격 변경허가 (status-change permit) under [Article 24 of the Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1000822779&chrClsCd=010202) before you can legally start work under the new category, with a **100,000 won** fee. Starting the new job before that approval comes through isn't a paperwork technicality you can fix later: [Article 94, item 12](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkProc.do?lsNm=%EC%B6%9C%EC%9E%85%EA%B5%AD%EA%B4%80%EB%A6%AC%EB%B2%95&joNo=009400&mode=11) sets a penalty of **up to 3 years in prison or a 30,000,000 won fine** for working outside your current status without permission — the same ceiling the law uses for overstaying.

## When it goes wrong

**You're approaching the 3-year cap without a job offer yet.** There's no further D-10 extension once you hit the cap — the law treats it as a hard limit, not a renewable-indefinitely category. Your options at that point are qualifying for a different status entirely (another degree program, a different visa route you're independently eligible for) or leaving Korea. Don't wait for your final renewal appointment to think through this; start considering alternatives with several months of runway left, not weeks.

**You got an offer, but the E-7 status-change approval hasn't come through yet and your new employer wants you to start.** Say no to starting early. The Article 94 penalty above applies regardless of how reasonable the employer's request sounds, and it's the kind of violation that follows you into future visa applications. Ask your new employer's HR team to confirm they understand the HiKorea timeline — reputable companies that sponsor E-7 hires deal with this regularly and shouldn't push back.

**Your internship at one company is approaching the 1-year single-company cap.** Under the pre-reform rule this didn't matter much since the total internship time across all companies was already capped at 1 year; under the current rule, the per-company cap is now the binding constraint if you're trying to extend time at a single place. Talk to your Employment Center or HiKorea caseworker before the cap hits about whether a formal employment offer (moving you off D-10 into a work status) is realistic, rather than assuming the internship can simply continue.

**Your point-system application or renewal gets rejected on a document issue.** Ask the caseworker exactly which document or score component failed before resubmitting — a rejection for missing or outdated paperwork is usually fixable within your existing timeline, but you need the specific reason, not a general "incomplete" note, to fix it correctly the first time.

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Confirmed which D-10 entry route you're on — Korean-university graduate vs. other — since the 3-year figure is specific to graduates</li>
  <li>Checked your current point-system score and pass line directly on HiKorea or by calling 1345</li>
  <li>If doing paid work beyond an approved internship: filed the 120,000 won 체류자격외활동허가 separately</li>
  <li>Filed renewal before expiry, budgeting the 60,000 won fee (or 20% less filing online)</li>
  <li>Did not start a new job before status-change approval came through</li>
  <li>Tracked the single-company internship cap of 1 year if doing an extended internship</li>
</ul>
