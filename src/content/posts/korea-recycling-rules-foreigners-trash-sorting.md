You carried your trash downstairs, and a neighbor stared at the bag like you had set something on fire. Korea recycling rules for foreigners are confusing at first, because you can’t just throw everything in one bin. This guide explains the bags, the sorting, and the timing, so you stop guessing.

## Short version

Korea charges you for trash by volume. General waste and food waste each go in their own official bag, and recyclables go out separately, clean, in a plain clear bag or the apartment’s bins. Put it out only from sunset to midnight, and check your district for the collection day.

## Why Korea recycling rules feel so strict

Seoul is serious about sorting. In January 2026, the [Korea Herald reported](https://m.koreaherald.com/article/10662903) that the city wants to cut household waste, and that officials plan spot checks in areas with a lot of badly sorted trash. Seoul also plans waste-sorting education in areas with large foreign resident populations, so you are not being singled out. The rules are just new to you.

The target is concrete, not just a slogan: the same report says the city wants to cut the amount of general waste each resident produces by 10 liters a year over the next 2 years, which it compares to about one standard trash bag per person.

## Step 1: Buy the right bags

Jung-gu’s [official English guide](https://junggu.seoul.kr/english/content.do?cmsid=14839) lists the bag types. General waste goes in a semi-transparent white volume-based bag. Food waste goes in a semi-transparent yellow bag made for food waste only. Recyclables go out in ordinary plain plastic bags.

The most important detail: bags are district-specific. The Jung-gu page says different districts use different bags and prices differ too, and the [Jung-gu general waste page](https://www.junggu.seoul.kr/english/content.do?cmsid=14840) tells you to double-check the district printed on the bag. Buy yours at a nearby supermarket or convenience store, and don’t carry bags over from a previous address.

Prices step up with bag size. Dobong-gu's [official 2026 fee table](https://www.dobong.go.kr/subsite/waste/Contents.asp?code=10007356) is a useful example: a 3-liter general-waste bag costs 90 won, 5 liters costs 130 won, 10 liters costs 250 won, 20 liters costs 490 won, 30 liters costs 740 won, 50 liters costs 1,250 won, 75 liters costs 1,880 won, and 100 liters costs 2,500 won. [News1](https://www.news1.kr/society/general-society/6113180) reported in March 2026 that the 20-liter price across Seoul has held at 490 won since a 2017 increase from 440 won, even though the full schedule for every other size still varies by district. If you just moved, [reporting your new address within 15 days](/report-address-change-korea-arc-15-days/) and switching to your new district's bags are two separate errands — don't skip either one.

**Jun’s tip:** When you move to a new gu, finish your old bags first or give them to a friend in the same district. The wrong district’s bag is not what your district expects.

## Step 2: Sort your food waste

Food waste is where most newcomers slip. Seoul’s [food waste disposal standard](https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-distributes-outline-of-food-waste-disposal-standard-in-ten-languages-for-foreign-residents/), distributed in ten languages, says to remove moisture and impurities before disposal. It also says to rinse salty foods such as kimchi and gochujang, and to chop big vegetables like whole radishes and cabbages, because long pieces can break the machines.

The same standard lists items that are NOT food waste, including:

- Bones of cows, pigs and chickens, and fish bones

- Shells of clams, oysters, crabs and eggs

- Hard shells of walnuts, chestnuts, peanuts and pineapples

- Peach and apricot seeds

- Onion, garlic and ginger skins

- Tea and herb grounds

- Chopsticks, toothpicks, plastic, foil, gum and bottle caps

Those items go in the general waste bag. The list is a Seoul-wide recommendation, so a few districts may differ slightly. If a rule surprises you, ask your district office.

**Jun’s tip:** Keep a small bowl on the counter for bones and shells while you cook. It saves you from digging through the yellow bag later.

Not every building uses a flat-price bag for food waste. Many apartments charge by weight or volume through an RFID-tagged bin instead, and Dobong-gu's [published rate](https://www.dobong.go.kr/subsite/waste/Contents.asp?code=10007356) shows how that math works: regular households pay 100 won per liter or 130 won per kilogram, a small restaurant pays 140 won per liter or 190 won per kilogram, and a high-volume business pays 170 won per liter or 230 won per kilogram. Check the sticker on your building's bin or the card reader screen — it tells you which rate you're being charged before you tap in.

## Step 3: Separate recyclables

Paper, cans, glass, plastic, vinyl and scrap metal are collected as recyclables, separated by material. The [Jung-gu recycling page](https://www.junggu.seoul.kr/english/content.do?cmsid=14842) says dirty vinyl can be mistaken for garbage, so collect clean vinyl in a transparent bag before putting it out. Empty and rinse containers first. Greasy or food-smeared items don’t count as recyclables.

In an apartment complex, you usually take recyclables to the recycling area on the designated day. In a detached house or villa, they go in front of your home.

## Step 4: Learn the timing

According to Jung-gu, you put waste out from sunset to midnight, and collection days differ by district. The [Jung-gu overview page](https://junggu.seoul.kr/english/content.do?cmsid=14839) says to ask your district (gu) office. Hours and days can differ by area, so check the notice board in your building or call the district.

Dobong-gu's own [posted schedule](https://www.dobong.go.kr/subsite/waste/Contents.asp?code=10007357) gives a concrete example: curbside pickup runs 18:00 to 24:00 every day, but general waste, food waste and recyclables don't go out the evening before a public holiday or on a Saturday, because Sundays and holidays aren't collection days. Bulky waste has its own skip list — no Friday or Saturday before a holiday either. A bag put out on a skipped night doesn't get collected sooner; it just sits on the curb overnight, which is exactly the kind of abandoned trash the city's spot checks are looking for.

Apartment buildings often have their own system, like a food waste machine or a bin in a fixed spot. Follow the building notice over any general guide.

## Step 5: Dispose of big items properly

Furniture and large appliances aren’t allowed in standard bags. The Jung-gu page lists drawers, desks, beds, sofas, TVs, refrigerators, washing machines and air conditioners as bulky waste, which you need to report to the district office first. Don’t leave a mattress on the sidewalk and hope for the best.

## When something goes wrong

What if you get it wrong? Most sorting mistakes still have a fix if you catch them before the next collection day.

**You used your old district's bag, or the wrong size.** Collectors only recognize their own gu's printed bag, so a bag from a different district can be left on the curb uncollected, or treated as illegal dumping if it sits there. If you just moved, use up your old bags at your old address, or hand them to a neighbor still living there, and buy your new gu's bags before you put anything else out.

**Your bag gets refused for mixed waste.** Dobong-gu's own guidance is blunt about this: if recyclables such as paper, vinyl or plastic end up inside a general-waste bag, the whole bag is refused, not just the recyclable item. If you forget to check before tying the bag shut, the fix is the same the next day: reopen it, sort it properly, and put it out again on the next scheduled day.

**You put trash out on a skipped night.** A bag left out the evening before a public holiday or on a Saturday just sits there uncollected — once that night's run is over, it's too late to get it picked up any sooner. Check your building's notice board for the holiday calendar before you carry anything downstairs.

**You get fined for illegal disposal.** The same Jung-gu page that lists the bag rules sets a penalty for illegal disposal — a fine of 100,000 won up to 1,000,000 won, depending on the district — and mixing food waste into general waste raises the rate. A fine notice usually names a window to pay or contest it at the district office, so call the number on the notice instead of ignoring it.

## Quick checklist

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Buy bags from your own district</li>
  <li>White bag for general waste, yellow bag for food waste only</li>
  <li>Drain food waste and remove bones, shells and skins</li>
  <li>Rinse recyclables and sort them by material</li>
  <li>Put trash out from sunset to midnight on your district’s day</li>
  <li>Report bulky items before dumping them</li>
</ul>

I checked these rules against official Seoul, Jung-gu and Dobong-gu pages, plus two independent news reports, in November 2026. Details and prices vary by district, so confirm your own gu’s collection days and fees.
