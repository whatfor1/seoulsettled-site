You landed, got your ARC, rented a car for a weekend trip, and then wondered: am I even allowed to drive here? The answer is "yes, for a while," and the clock starts earlier than most people think. This guide explains how driving in Korea as a foreigner works, and how to exchange your foreign license for a Korean one.

I’m Jun, a Seoul local, and I wrote this by reading the government’s own legal guide. I link every rule to the page where I found it, so you can double-check.

## Can you drive in Korea with a foreign license?

Short version: you can drive with either an international driving permit (IDP) or a "mutually recognized" foreign license, but only for one year from your date of entry. This comes from the Road Traffic Act, as summarized on [Korea’s Easy Law portal](https://m.easylaw.go.kr/MOM/SubCsmOvRetrieve.laf?ccfNo=1&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1&csmSeq=2332&langCd=700101).

Three details matter:

- The one year counts from the day you entered Korea, not the day you got your ARC.

- You may only drive the vehicle types written on your license or permit.

- Commercial vehicles are off limits, although rental cars are allowed.

Driving without a valid license or permit counts as unlicensed driving. The penalty on that page is up to one year in prison or a fine of up to KRW 3,000,000. Not worth the risk.

## IDP or recognized license: which one do you have?

An IDP is issued by your home country’s licensing authority under the 1949 Geneva Convention, the 1968 Vienna Convention, or a bilateral agreement. Ask your home authority about getting one before you fly.

A mutually recognized license means Korea and your country accept each other’s licenses. The Easy Law page lists the countries as of August 2026. Some US states, for example, are on the list, but the page notes that Oregon, Idaho and Utah require a written test.

Jun’s tip: carry the physical license or permit every time you drive. Not carrying it can bring a fine of up to KRW 200,000, even if you are fully licensed.

## How to exchange your foreign license for a Korean license

If you plan to stay longer than a year, exchange your license. You apply in person at any driver’s license examination office. Proxy applications are not allowed (except for certain ID card holders from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs).

The procedure has three steps:

1. Document review, including embassy verification of your license.

2. An aptitude test, which is a physical exam, plus a written test if your country is not recognized.

3. Issuance of your Korean license.

If your country is recognized, the written test is waived. If it is not recognized, you take a written test with a KRW 10,000 fee.

## Documents you need to bring

For a foreign national, the Easy Law guide lists:

- Your original foreign driver’s license

- Original passport, showing continuous travel records

- Original residence card (your ARC)

- Three color photos taken within six months, 3.5 x 4.5 cm

- An embassy certificate or an Apostille for your license

- A certificate of entry and exit records, from your birth year to today

If you do not have an ARC yet, read my guide to [getting your ARC in Korea](/how-to-get-arc-korea/) first.

Jun’s tip: the embassy certificate or Apostille is the step that delays people, because it has to be arranged before you go to the office. Ask your embassy how long it takes, and only then book a day off. Also note that the Korea Road Traffic Authority says the documents vary by applicant, so check its site before you go.

## What does the exchange cost?

The issuance fee on the guide is KRW 15,000 for a mobile IC license (Korean and English), or KRW 10,000 for a general card. The written test, if required, is KRW 10,000. Photo and medical fees may be extra, so bring some cash and a card.

## Quick checklist before you go

- Confirm whether your country is recognized.

- Order the embassy certificate or Apostille.

- Get your entry and exit record certificate.

- Take your passport, ARC, original license and three photos.

- Go in person to a driver’s license examination office.

- Confirm details on the [Road Traffic Authority site](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/).

## Common mistakes

Many people wait until month eleven and discover the embassy paperwork takes weeks. Others bring a copy instead of an original. And a provisional license or learner’s permit generally cannot be exchanged, so make sure yours is a full license.
