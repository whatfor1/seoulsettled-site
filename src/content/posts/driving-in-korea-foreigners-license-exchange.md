You landed, got your ARC, rented a car for a weekend trip, and then wondered: am I even allowed to drive here? The answer is "yes, for a while," and the clock starts earlier than most people think. This guide explains how driving in Korea as a foreigner works, how to exchange your foreign license for a Korean one, and what it actually costs.

I'm Jun, a Seoul local, and I wrote this by reading the government's own legal guide and the licensing authority's own exchange page. I link every rule to the page where I found it, so you can double-check.

**Never held a license anywhere, in any country?** This guide is about exchanging an existing foreign license — if you don't have one to exchange, you need Korea's full first-time process instead, which I cover in my [guide to getting a Korean license from scratch](/korean-drivers-license-from-scratch-foreigners/).

## Can you drive in Korea with a foreign license?

Short version: you can drive with either an international driving permit (IDP) or a "mutually recognized" foreign license, but only for 1 year from your date of entry. This comes from the Road Traffic Act, as summarized on [Korea's Easy Law portal](https://m.easylaw.go.kr/MOM/SubCsmOvRetrieve.laf?ccfNo=1&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1&csmSeq=2332&langCd=700101).

Three details matter:

- The 1-year window counts from the day you entered Korea, not the day you got your ARC.

- You may only drive the vehicle types written on your license or permit.

- Commercial vehicles are off limits, although rental cars are allowed.

Driving without a valid license or permit counts as unlicensed driving. The penalty on that page is up to 1 year in prison or a fine of up to 3,000,000 won. Not worth the risk.

## IDP or recognized license: which one do you have?

An IDP is issued by your home country's licensing authority under the 1949 Geneva Convention, the 1968 Vienna Convention, or a bilateral agreement. Ask your home authority about getting one before you fly.

A mutually recognized license means Korea and your country accept each other's licenses. The Easy Law page lists the countries as of August 2026. Some US states, for example, are on the list, but the page notes that Oregon, Idaho and Utah require a written test.

Jun's tip: carry the physical license or permit every time you drive. Not carrying it can bring a fine of up to 200,000 won, even if you are fully licensed.

## How to exchange your foreign license for a Korean license

If you plan to stay longer than 1 year, exchange your license. You apply in person at any driver's license examination office. Proxy applications are not allowed (except for certain ID card holders from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs).

The procedure has three steps:

1. Document review, including embassy verification of your license.

2. An aptitude test, which is a physical exam, plus a written test if your country is not recognized.

3. Issuance of your Korean license.

If your country is recognized, the written test is waived. If it is not recognized, you take a written test with a 10,000 won fee, available in Korean, English, Chinese or Vietnamese according to the [Road Traffic Authority's exchange guide](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/diGuide/selectDiGuide05.do).

## Documents you need to bring

For a foreign national, the Easy Law guide and the [Road Traffic Authority's own exchange page](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/diGuide/selectDiGuide05.do) both list:

- Your original foreign driver's license

- Original passport, showing continuous travel records

- Original residence card (your ARC)

- Three color photos taken within 6 months, 3.5 x 4.5 cm

- An embassy certificate or an [Apostille](/apostille-documents-korea-foreigners/) for your license

- A certificate of entry and exit records, from your birth year to today

If you do not have an ARC yet, read my guide to [getting your ARC in Korea](/how-to-get-arc-korea/) first.

Jun's tip: the embassy certificate or Apostille is the step that delays people, because it has to be arranged before you go to the office. Ask your embassy how long it takes, and only then book a day off — my [guide to apostilles and document authentication](/apostille-documents-korea-foreigners/) covers which path applies depending on where you're from. The Road Traffic Authority's own page also warns that the exact document list varies by applicant category (which country issued your license, and whether you're a foreign national or an overseas Korean), so double-check which column applies to you before you go.

## What does the exchange cost?

The issuance fee on both official pages is 15,000 won for a mobile IC license (Korean and English), or 10,000 won for a general card. The written test, if required, is 10,000 won. Photo and medical fees may be extra, so bring some cash and a card.

## When something goes wrong

**Your embassy certificate or Apostille gets rejected or delayed.** This is the single biggest cause of a wasted trip, since the office can't review your file without it. If your embassy's own processing takes longer than expected, your driving privilege on the foreign license still ends at the 1-year mark regardless — book the embassy step as early as possible, not the week before your deadline.

**The office says your documents don't match your applicant category.** The Road Traffic Authority's exchange page lists different document sets depending on your nationality and whether your country is "recognized." If you bring the wrong set, you'll be sent home to gather the right one rather than being allowed to submit something close enough.

**Your country isn't on the recognized list.** You still qualify to exchange, but you'll need to pass the 10,000 won written test in addition to the physical exam — plan for an extra visit if you don't pass the written portion the first time, since the page doesn't guarantee same-day retakes.

**You only have a provisional license or learner's permit.** These generally cannot be exchanged. If that's what you have, you'll need to go through Korea's first-time licensing process instead; see my [guide to getting a Korean license from scratch](/korean-drivers-license-from-scratch-foreigners/).

## Quick checklist before you go

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Confirm whether your country is recognized.</li>
  <li>Order the embassy certificate or Apostille.</li>
  <li>Get your entry and exit record certificate.</li>
  <li>Take your passport, ARC, original license and three photos.</li>
  <li>Bring 10,000–15,000 won for the issuance fee, plus 10,000 won if a written test applies.</li>
  <li>Go in person to a driver's license examination office.</li>
  <li>Confirm details on the [Road Traffic Authority site](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/).</li>
</ul>

## Common mistakes

Many people wait until month eleven and discover the embassy paperwork takes weeks. Others bring a copy instead of an original. And a provisional license or learner's permit generally cannot be exchanged, so make sure yours is a full license.
