A line on your payslip labeled 국민연금 (National Pension) is the first thing most foreign employees notice and the last thing most of them understand. It's not optional, the rate just rose for the first time since 1998, and almost nobody explains the one part that actually matters to someone who isn't staying in Korea forever: you can usually get the money back when you leave.

## Who has to join, and who doesn't

The National Pension Service's own foreigner guidance is specific: a foreigner between 18 and 60 working at an NPS-covered workplace becomes a 사업장가입자 (workplace-insured member) automatically, through payroll, the same way a Korean coworker would. A foreigner in that age range who isn't on a covered payroll but lives in Korea generally becomes a 지역가입자 (individually insured member) instead, per the same [NPS foreigner-enrollment guidance](https://www.nps.or.kr/pnsinfo/ntpsklg/getOHAF0035M0.do).

A few groups are carved out: exchange students and trainees, diplomats and their families, and foreigners whose home country doesn't extend an equivalent pension to Korean nationals under a reciprocity rule. Workers sent to Korea from a country with a social security agreement can also skip Korean enrollment by submitting proof they're still covered at home. Korea has signed agreements covering a long list of countries, and the exemption terms differ agreement by agreement, so if you were posted to Korea by an overseas employer, check your specific situation against the [NPS foreigner guidance](https://www.nps.or.kr/pnsinfo/ntpsklg/getOHAF0035M0.do) rather than assuming either way.

## The rate just changed: 9% to 9.5%

For 28 years, Korea's National Pension contribution rate held flat at 9% of your 기준소득월액 (standard monthly income). That changed on **January 1, 2026**: under the pension reform law passed by the National Assembly (법률 제20903호, amending [국민연금법 제88조](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?mid=a10503000000&bid=0027&list_no=1485039&act=view)), the rate steps up **0.5 percentage points every year starting 2026**, reaching **13% by 2033**. For 2026, that puts the rate at **9.5%**. If you're a workplace-insured employee, you and your employer split it evenly — **4.75% each** — deducted straight from your paycheck.

The 9.5% doesn't apply to your full salary without limit. NPS caps the standard monthly income used for the calculation: as of **July 2026**, the ceiling is **6,590,000 won** and the floor is **410,000 won** a month, adjusted from 6,370,000 won and 400,000 won six months earlier to reflect a 3.4% rise in the average contributor's income. At the ceiling, the maximum combined monthly contribution comes to **626,050 won**, split to about 313,025 won from your own paycheck if you're an employee.

## When you get it back: the lump-sum refund

This is the part most foreign workers never ask about. Under [국민연금법 제77조](https://lbox.kr/statute/%EA%B5%AD%EB%AF%BC%EC%97%B0%EA%B8%88%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C77%EC%A1%B0), a 반환일시금 (lump-sum refund) is available to anyone who paid into the National Pension and then either turns 60 with under 10 years of contributions, dies without triggering a survivor pension, or **loses Korean nationality or moves abroad for good**. That third trigger is the one that applies to most foreigners leaving Korea permanently, regardless of how long they contributed.

Foreign nationality adds one more layer on top of Article 77, confirmed on the NPS's own [English-language lump-sum refund page](https://www.nps.or.kr/eng/ntnlpnsplan/frgnrlpsmrfnd/getOHAI0015M0.do): you qualify if your home country grants Korean nationals an equivalent benefit, if Korea has a social security agreement with your country that specifically covers lump-sum refunds, or if your contributions were made under an **E-8, E-9, or H-2** visa — those three visa categories get the refund regardless of nationality. A short list of major nationalities (the U.K., Japan, China, and the Netherlands among them) have exemption-only agreements with Korea and generally can't claim this refund on an ordinary work visa, so check your specific country against the NPS page rather than assuming you qualify.

The payout is every won you contributed — plus the employer's matching share, if you were a workplace-insured employee — plus interest calculated at the 3-year fixed-deposit rate for the relevant period, which NPS sets at **2.2% for 2026**.

## How to actually claim it

NPS lays out three ways to file, per its [official refund guidance](https://www.nps.or.kr/pnsinfo/ntpsklg/getOHAF0079M0.do) and the associated [gov.kr service listing](https://www.gov.kr/mw/AA020InfoCappView.do?CappBizCD=14600000303):

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Visit any NPS branch in Korea before you leave, with your refund application, passport, and alien registration card, and have the money sent to your bank account</li>
  <li>Apply at an NPS branch within 1 month of your departure date for the airport payment service, then collect the cash in person at Incheon Airport Terminal 1's arrival hall, Exit A2, at the airport counseling center (open 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.)</li>
  <li>File by mail from overseas after you've already left — this requires your documents to be notarized in your country of residence and then authenticated by a Korean consulate (or Apostille-certified, for convention countries), which makes it the slowest and most paperwork-heavy of the three</li>
</ul>

Either way you'll need proof you're leaving within a month (a flight ticket or booking confirmation), your ARC, and bank details — a passbook copy for a Korean account, or a statement showing your name, account number, and bank code in English for an overseas one. Once approved, NPS says the money typically reaches your account within about 3 to 5 business days, with the whole claim usually wrapped up within a month.

## When it goes wrong: deadlines missed and claims denied

**If you let the deadline pass.** The right to claim a lump-sum refund for losing nationality or moving abroad lapses after **5 years** from the date you became eligible, under [국민연금법 제115조](https://www.nps.or.kr/pnsinfo/ntpsklg/getOHAF0048M0.do?menuId=MN24001117&tab=tab8). That 5-year clock is specific to this trigger — a separate 10-year window that gets quoted online applies only to the age-60-with-under-10-years-contributions case, not to people who leave the country, so don't assume you have longer than you do. File before you board your flight out, or soon after, rather than treating it as something to deal with later.

**If your claim is denied or the amount looks wrong.** You can file a 심사청구 (request for examination) with NPS under [국민연금법 제108조](https://lbox.kr/v2/statute/%EA%B5%AD%EB%AF%BC%EC%97%B0%EA%B8%88%EB%B2%95/%EB%B3%B8%EB%AC%B8%20%3E%20%EC%A0%9C7%EC%9E%A5%20%3E%20%EC%A0%9C108%EC%A1%B0) within 90 days of learning about the decision, or within 180 days of the decision itself if you learned about it late. NPS has to decide within 60 days, extendable once by 30 more. If that result still isn't right, the next step is a 재심사청구 (request for re-examination) to the Ministry of Health and Welfare's National Pension Re-examination Committee.

**If you already left and missed your window to apply in person.** The mail-from-abroad path above still works, even years later, as long as you're inside the 5-year limit — it's just slower, so build in time for notarization and consular authentication before you count on the money landing.
