An employer who never registered for industrial accident insurance still owes an injured worker the same benefits as one who did. Korea's 산업재해보상보험법 (Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Act) makes coverage automatic — 당연적용 — for almost every workplace with one or more employees, a rule that has applied since July 1, 2000. Nothing in the law's coverage article, [Article 6](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/산업재해보상보험법/제6조), mentions nationality or visa status, and the Ministry of Employment and Labor says so directly: "산재보험은 일하는 근로자의 업무상 재해 보호를 위한 것으로 국적 및 체류신분에 따른 보호의 차별을 두고 있지 않습니다" (industrial accident insurance protects working employees from work-related injury and does not discriminate by nationality or residency status) — stated in a [2022 MOEL explainer](https://www.moel.go.kr/news/enews/explain/enewsView.do?news_seq=14067). This guide covers what the system actually pays in 2026, how to file without your employer's involvement, and what to do if an employer pressures you to skip it.

## Coverage doesn't depend on your paperwork — or your employer's

The Korean Supreme Court has gone further than the statute's plain text. In [대법원 1995. 9. 15. 선고 94누12067 판결](https://www.law.go.kr), the court held that a foreign worker in a de facto employment relationship is entitled to 요양급여 (medical treatment benefits) even without valid work authorization — the insurance protects the underlying work relationship, not the paperwork around it.

That logic extends to employer registration, too. If an employer never signed up for industrial accident insurance, or signed up but fell behind on premiums, the injured worker's claim isn't affected — coverage is automatic by law, not by enrollment. The employer is the one who pays for the gap after the fact: under [시행령 제34조 of the premium collection act](https://www.law.go.kr), an employer who never registered gets billed a 50% surcharge on top of the unpaid premium (capped at 5 times the amount owed), and one who registered but fell into arrears gets a 10% surcharge, with exceptions if 50% or more of the premium was already paid. Either way, that's a bill for the employer to deal with — not a reason for a worker to go without treatment or lost-income pay.

## Filing doesn't need your employer's sign-off

[Article 41](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/산업재해보상보험법/제41조) sets out who can file a 요양급여 (medical treatment benefit) application, and an employer's consent or signature appears nowhere in it. The injured worker files directly with 근로복지공단 (the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service, comwel) — the same agency that runs the [간이대지급금 fund covering unpaid wages and severance](/unpaid-wages-korea-foreign-workers/). The treating hospital or clinic can also submit the application on the worker's behalf, but only with the worker's own consent — it isn't a channel for an employer to control the process either.

In practice you have a few ways to file:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Online through comwel's own portal at total.comwel.or.kr, which requires a 공동인증서 (a government-recognized digital certificate) to log in</li>
  <li>In person at any regional comwel branch, with the treating hospital's medical opinion attached</li>
  <li>Through the treating hospital or clinic directly, if you consent to let them file for you</li>
</ul>

For phone support, comwel's general line is **1588-0075** — note that the published line doesn't list foreign-language service, so bring someone who can translate or call from a hospital's international desk if your Korean isn't there yet. Comwel also runs a dedicated Vietnamese-language industrial-accident line at **032-540-4740** for exactly this reason, which tells you the gap is real enough that comwel built around it for at least one language. If what you need is broader multilingual guidance rather than comwel-specific filing help, HRD Korea's EPS counseling center at **1577-0071** covers 18 languages, but it's a separate agency built around the Employment Permit System generally — not a comwel hotline — so treat it as a first stop for orientation, not a substitute for filing with comwel itself.

## What the benefits actually pay in 2026

<ul class="checklist">
  <li><strong>요양급여 (medical treatment):</strong> covers the cost of treatment for the work-related injury or illness itself, paid to the hospital rather than to you directly</li>
  <li><strong>휴업급여 (lost income):</strong> 70% of your average daily wage for each day you can't work because of the injury, under <a href="https://www.law.go.kr/법령/산업재해보상보험법/제52조">Article 52</a> — but the article sets the benefit at zero if the time off is 3 days or less, so short absences are covered through 요양급여 only, not lost-income pay</li>
  <li><strong>장해급여 (permanent disability):</strong> paid once your condition is medically stable, graded 1 through 14 by severity. Grades 1-3 are pension-only, grades 4-7 let the worker choose between a pension or a lump sum, and grades 8-14 are lump-sum only. If you're a foreign worker who has already left Korea and no longer resides here, the lump-sum option is what you get regardless of grade, under <a href="https://www.law.go.kr">Article 57</a> and its enforcement decree</li>
  <li><strong>유족급여 (survivor benefits):</strong> paid to dependents if the injury is fatal — the exact share depends on how many dependents qualify and their relationship to the worker, which comwel calculates case by case rather than off a single published rate. Confirm your specific case through comwel rather than a general guide</li>
  <li><strong>장례비 (funeral expenses):</strong> set at 120 days of the worker's average wage, but bounded by an annual floor and ceiling. For 2026, that range is <strong>13,943,000 won to 19,279,760 won</strong>, per <a href="https://www.easylaw.go.kr">고용노동부고시 제2025-85호</a></li>
</ul>

The wage floor underneath 휴업급여 also matters for lower earners: [Article 54](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/산업재해보상보험법/제54조) bumps the rate from 70% up to 90% of average wage when 70% would land at or below 80% of that year's 최저보상기준금액 (minimum compensation base amount) — though the 90% payout itself is capped at 80% of the 최저보상기준금액. For 2026, the 최저보상기준금액 is **82,560 won per day**, and the maximum compensation base (최고보상기준금액, which caps how high 휴업급여 can run for high earners) is **268,299 won per day**, both set by [고용노동부고시 제2025-84호](https://www.easylaw.go.kr), effective January 1 through December 31, 2026.

## The 3-year clock — and the one that's 5

[Article 112](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/산업재해보상보험법/제112조) sets a general statute of limitations of **3 years** for most benefit claims, running from the date the right to claim arises. Four benefit types get longer: 장해급여, 유족급여, 장례비, and the pneumoconiosis-specific 진폐보상연금 and 진폐유족연금 all get **5 years** instead. [Article 113](https://www.law.go.kr) adds one protection worth knowing: filing a claim interrupts (tolls) the clock, including for related benefit claims where a work-relatedness determination is involved — so filing one claim on time can preserve your ability to file a related one later, rather than each needing to beat the deadline independently on its own.

## When it goes wrong

**Your employer pushes 공상처리 instead of letting you file.** 공상처리 — a private, off-the-books settlement paid directly by the employer instead of going through comwel — is only officially sanctioned by the Ministry of Employment and Labor for injuries needing under 4 days of treatment. Using it to quietly handle an injury that actually needs 4 or more days of treatment is exactly the pattern MOEL warns against, because it routes around the benefits above entirely. If you're pressured into it for anything beyond a very minor injury, you're giving up 요양급여, 휴업급여, and a 장해급여 claim if the injury turns out worse than expected.

**You're told you'll be fired or treated worse if you file.** [Article 111-2](https://www.law.go.kr) states plainly that an employer may not dismiss a worker or otherwise disadvantage them because the worker filed an insurance claim. Violating it carries a criminal penalty under [Article 127, paragraph 3](https://www.law.go.kr): up to **2 years in prison or a 20,000,000 won fine** for the employer. Report retaliation to comwel or the local labor office rather than letting it go — the law treats it as a separate offense from the injury itself.

**Your employer tries to hide the accident instead of reporting it.** Concealing a workplace accident is a separate violation under 산업안전보건법 (the Occupational Safety and Health Act, a different statute from the insurance act), carrying up to 1 year in prison or a 10,000,000 won fine for the employer. You can still file your own claim directly with comwel regardless of whether your employer ever reported the accident.

**Your employer says you're not covered because they never registered you.** As covered above, that's not how 당연적용 works — your coverage doesn't depend on your employer's registration status, and the unpaid-premium surcharge is the employer's problem to sort out with comwel, not yours.

**Your claim gets denied.** Comwel issues a written decision with its reasoning. You can request a review through comwel's own 심사청구 process, and from there an appeal to the separate 산업재해보상보험재심사위원회 (Industrial Accident Compensation Insurance Reexamination Committee), or pursue it through the courts — the 1995 Supreme Court precedent above is exactly the kind of case where a first-instance denial over work-authorization status didn't hold up on appeal.
