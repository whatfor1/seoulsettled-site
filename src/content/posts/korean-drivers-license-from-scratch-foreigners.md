Eighteen is the minimum age Korea's Road Traffic Act sets for a regular driver's license, and it applies the same way to a foreigner with an ARC as it does to a Korean citizen — nationality changes almost nothing about this process. What changes everything is whether you already hold a foreign license. If you do, you exchange it; I cover that in a [separate guide](/driving-in-korea-foreigners-license-exchange/). If you've never held a license anywhere, in any country, you go through Korea's full first-time process instead: six steps, three real tests, and your Alien Registration Card as the document that gets you in the door.

This guide walks through that process in order, with every fee and passing score checked against Korea Road Traffic Authority (도로교통공단) pages and the government's plain-language legal summaries. I'm Jun, a Seoul local, and I'm writing this for the readers who emailed after the exchange guide asking what to do when there's no foreign license to exchange.

## Who this is for, and what you need before you start

This route is for anyone applying for a Class 1 or Class 2 regular license (1종/2종 보통) who has never held a driving license in any country. The minimum age is 18, per the [Road Traffic Act's plain-language summary on easylaw.go.kr](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMain.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=668&ccfNo=2&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1), run by Korea's Ministry of Government Legislation.

Before you can file anything, you need:

- **A completed alien registration.** The official application-requirements page on [gov.kr](https://www.gov.kr/mw/AA020InfoCappView.do?CappBizCD=13200000053) lists either a **외국인등록사실증명** (certificate of alien registration) or, for overseas Koreans, a **국내거소신고사실증명** as a required document. You can only get this certificate after you've completed [ARC registration](/how-to-get-arc-korea/) — there's no shortcut around having it first.

- **Your passport**, plus a copy.

- **One color photo**, 3.5 cm × 4.5 cm, the same spec used on the ARC itself.

A first-time application for a Class 1 or Class 2 regular license is filed in person — either at a police station or at a driver's license examination office (운전면허시험장) — not online. You can look up your nearest exam office and reserve test slots through the Road Traffic Authority's civil-affairs site, [safedriving.or.kr](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/).

## The six-step order, and what each one actually tests

Korea's process runs in a fixed sequence — you can't take the written test before the safety class, or the road test before the functional test. Here's the order, confirmed against the [easylaw.go.kr process summary](https://www.easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=668&ccfNo=2&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1) and the Road Traffic Authority's own fee and test pages.

### 1. Traffic safety education — free, 1 hour

Every first-time applicant must complete **1 hour** of traffic safety education before sitting the written test. The [government service listing](https://www.gov.kr/portal/service/serviceInfo/PTR000050209) is explicit that "수강료는 무료이고" — tuition is free — and that it's a mandatory audiovisual class you can reserve online through safedriving.or.kr or at the exam office in person. Online booking closes the day before class; there's no same-day signup.

### 2. Physical exam (적성검사) — 7,000–8,000 KRW

A short physical check confirms your vision and color recognition are good enough to drive. According to the Road Traffic Authority's [published fee schedule](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/guide/rerGuide07View.do?menuCode=MN-PO-1117), this costs **7,000 KRW** for most license classes, or **8,000 KRW** if you're going for a Class 1 large or special vehicle license.

### 3. Written test (학과시험) — 10,000 KRW, pass with 60–70 points

This is the step most foreigners worry about, and it's also the easiest to prepare for. The Road Traffic Authority's [written-test page](https://www.safedriving.or.kr/dtGuide/selectDtGuide08.do) sets out exactly what you're facing:

- **40 multiple-choice questions in 40 minutes.**

- **Passing score: 70 out of 100 for a Class 1 regular license, 60 out of 100 for Class 2 regular.**

- **Available in Korean, English, Chinese and Vietnamese** — so you don't need to study Korean vocabulary to pass, only traffic rules.

- **Fee: 10,000 KRW.**

A passed written test stays valid for **1 year**, during which you need to finish the functional and road tests — the same easylaw.go.kr page confirms the result expires after that window, so don't pass the written test and then let it sit.

### 4. Functional test (기능시험) — 25,000 KRW, pass with 80 points

Run on a closed test course, this checks basic car control: starting, stopping, parking, and tight turns, rather than real traffic. The Road Traffic Authority's fee page lists it at **25,000 KRW** for Class 1 and Class 2 regular licenses, with a passing score of **80 out of 100**.

Passing this test gets you a **provisional license, valid for 1 year**, which is what lets you legally practice on real roads before the final test.

### 5. Road test (도로주행시험) — 30,000 KRW, pass with 70 points

The last test happens on public roads with an examiner in the car, and costs **30,000 KRW**, with a passing score of **70 out of 100**.

### 6. License issuance — 10,000 or 15,000 KRW

Once you clear all three tests, the card itself costs **10,000 KRW** for a standard plastic license, or **15,000 KRW** for the mobile digital version (both bilingual, Korean and English).

## If you fail a test — the retake rules

Korea Road Traffic Authority's own exam-office notice spells out the waiting period for each test, and it's different for the written test than for the other two:

- **Written test:** retake from the **next day** ("불합격 후 익일 응시가능," per the [exam-office notice](https://www.koroad.or.kr/main/board/9/69920/board_view.do?cp=7&listType=list&bcstIdx1=70&bcstIdx2=97&bdOpenYn=Y&bdNoticeYn=N)).

- **Functional test and road test:** retake **3 days later, counting the fail date** — fail on a Monday, and the earliest retake is Thursday.

Pay the test fee again each time you retake; nothing here is free on a second attempt except the safety class, which you only sit once.

## Driving school, or doing it alone

You don't have to use a driving academy (학원) — you can book every test yourself directly through safedriving.or.kr and the exam office, and several guides to this process put the total cost of doing it that way at roughly **70,000 KRW** across all the fees above. Academies exist for people who want structured instruction before the functional and road tests, particularly if you've genuinely never sat behind a wheel. There's no single official academy price list, because tuition is set by each private academy rather than regulated centrally — call a couple of academies near you and compare quotes rather than trusting a round number from any guide, including this one.

## When it goes wrong

**If your application is rejected for a missing document.** The most common reason is showing up without the 외국인등록사실증명 — bring a copy as backup along with the original, and request a fresh certificate from an immigration office or via Government24 (정부24) if yours is more than a few weeks old.

**If your written test passes but your schedule falls apart.** You have exactly 1 year from the written test to finish the functional and road tests. If that year runs out, the written test no longer counts and you start again from step 3.

**If you fail the functional or road test more than once.** There's no cap on attempts, but each one costs the test fee again — 25,000 KRW for the functional test, 30,000 KRW for the road test — so repeated failures add up fast. If you're consistently struggling with the road test specifically, that's usually the signal to book a few private lessons rather than keep paying for retakes cold.
