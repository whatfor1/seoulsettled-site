Being fired in Korea doesn't automatically mean it was legal. Under the Labor Standards Act, an employer can't dismiss you without a justifiable reason (정당한 이유), and if they skip that reason — or skip the paperwork — you have a real, free, nationality-blind path to get your job back or get paid for the time you lost. The catch is that every step of that path runs on a short clock, and missing one of them can end the case before anyone looks at the merits.

## Who this actually covers

The protection in [근로기준법 제23조](https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C23%EC%A1%B0) — "an employer may not dismiss, suspend, transfer, reduce the pay of, or otherwise punish a worker without justifiable cause" — only reaches workplaces covered by the Act at all, and that depends on headcount. [근로기준법 제11조](https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C11%EC%A1%B0) applies the law's full protections — including the Labor Relations Commission remedy described below — to "any business or workplace that ordinarily employs **5 or more workers**." Below that, the Commission route isn't available, and a worker instead has to file a civil suit asking a court to declare the dismissal invalid (해고무효확인소송), which is a different, slower, and not-free process.

Nationality is not a factor anywhere in this law. Any worker — any visa type, any passport — at a covered workplace can use the same remedy a Korean national would.

## The 30-day notice rule

Separate from whether a dismissal is justified at all, [근로기준법 제26조](https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C26%EC%A1%B0) requires an employer to give **at least 30 days'** advance notice before any dismissal (including layoffs for business reasons), or pay **at least 30 days' worth of ordinary wages (통상임금)** instead. There are exactly three exceptions:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>You've worked continuously for less than 3 months</li>
  <li>A natural disaster or other unavoidable event makes it impossible to keep running the business</li>
  <li>You intentionally caused major disruption or property damage to the business, under standards set by Ministry of Employment and Labor regulation</li>
</ul>

Outside those three, skipping both the notice and the pay-in-lieu is itself a separate violation from an unjust dismissal — you can be owed the 30 days' pay even in a case where the firing itself turns out to have been justified.

## Filing for reinstatement — the 3-month deadline

[근로기준법 제28조](https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C28%EC%A1%B0) gives you **3 months from the date of the unjust dismissal** to apply to the Labor Relations Commission (노동위원회) for a remedy. This is calendar time, not working days, and there is no grace period written into the statute — file one day late and the case gets thrown out on procedure (각하) without anyone evaluating whether the dismissal was actually unjust.

Per [gov.kr's official guidance on filing](https://www.gov.kr/mw/AA020InfoCappView.do?CappBizCD=14900000440), the application itself can be submitted **online, in person, by fax, or by mail**, there is **no filing fee**, and typical processing from filing to judgment runs about **90 days**. Workers whose average monthly wage is under **3,000,000 KRW** are eligible for **free, certified labor-attorney (공인노무사) representation** through the Commission, confirmed on [easylaw.go.kr's foreign-worker labor relations guide](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=514&ccfNo=5&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1) — so cost is rarely the reason not to file.

## What the Commission can order

If the Commission finds the dismissal was unjust, [근로기준법 제30조](https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C30%EC%A1%B0) lets it order one of two things: **reinstatement to your original position (원직복직)**, or — if you don't actually want your old job back — **monetary compensation at least equal to the wages you would have earned during the dismissal period**. You choose which remedy you're asking for; the Commission doesn't force reinstatement on a worker who'd rather take the money and move on.

## If the employer just ignores the order

A remedial order isn't a suggestion. Under [근로기준법 제33조](https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C33%EC%A1%B0), an employer who fails to comply by the compliance deadline can be fined up to **30,000,000 KRW (3천만원)** per instance of non-compliance. The Commission must give the employer **30 days' written notice** before actually imposing the fine, and it can repeat the fine **up to twice a year, for up to 2 years**, counted from the date of the original order, until the employer actually complies.

## The appeal chain, if either side disputes the ruling

[근로기준법 제31조](https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C31%EC%A1%B0) sets two more deadlines on top of the initial 3-month filing window:

- **10 days** from being notified of the regional Labor Relations Commission's ruling, to appeal (재심) to the Central Labor Relations Commission (중앙노동위원회).
- **15 days** from being served the Central Commission's re-examination ruling, to file an administrative lawsuit (행정소송).

Skip both windows and whatever ruling you have — the original order, a dismissal of your case, or the re-examination result — becomes final either way, for either side.

## If you're an E-9 or H-2 worker: check the Article 25 angle too

If your dismissal came with the employer shutting down, cancelling your hiring permit, running an illegal dormitory, or withholding wages, that same set of facts may also qualify as "employer fault" under Article 25(4) of the Act on Employment of Foreign Workers — which matters separately from the unfair-dismissal remedy above. See my [E-9 workplace change guide](/e9-visa-change-workplace-korea-foreign-workers/) for the full list of employer-fault grounds: a workplace change caused by employer fault doesn't count against your 3-time (or 2-time, in the re-employment extension) change limit, on top of whatever you win at the Labor Commission.

## A caution specific to foreign workers

A certified figure worth knowing before your hearing: a human-rights group (희망을만드는법) filed a petition with the National Human Rights Commission on August 3, 2026, stating that over the roughly 5 years from 2021 through July 2026, the Labor Relations Commission provided interpreter or translation support in only **25 cases**, even though foreign workers filed **307** unfair-dismissal remedy applications in 2026 alone through August — and that the Commission has no dedicated multilingual counselor and no separate budget or standard for interpreter support, [per Nate News's report on the petition](https://news.nate.com/view/20260804n29030). There's no statutory right to a free interpreter at your hearing. If your Korean isn't strong enough to follow a hearing in real time, raise it with your caseworker or your 공인노무사 as early as possible and ask what support — if any — is available in your specific case, rather than assuming it's provided automatically.

## When it goes wrong

**You're past the 3-month filing deadline.** There is no exception written into 제28조 for this one — the case will be dismissed on procedure regardless of how strong the underlying dismissal claim is. If you're unsure exactly when the "date of dismissal" was (a verbal firing, an end-of-contract non-renewal, a forced resignation), get a 공인노무사 consultation immediately rather than waiting to be sure — the clock doesn't pause while you figure that out.

**Your workplace has fewer than 5 employees.** The Labor Commission route in this article doesn't apply to you. You still may have a claim, but it goes through a civil court filing (해고무효확인소송) instead, which is a different process with its own cost and timeline — a 공인노무사 or legal aid center can tell you whether it's worth pursuing for your situation.

**The Commission orders reinstatement or compensation and the employer still doesn't pay or take you back.** Report the non-compliance to the Commission — you can do this within 15 days after the compliance deadline passes — which can trigger the 30,000,000 KRW fine process above. This is a Commission enforcement issue, not something you negotiate with the employer directly.

**Your application gets rejected for a paperwork problem**, not on the merits. Ask the caseworker exactly which document or detail was deficient — a procedural rejection is often fixable on resubmission, but only if you're still inside the 3-month window, so don't let back-and-forth with the caseworker eat into your remaining time.

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Confirmed your workplace ordinarily employs 5 or more workers</li>
  <li>Noted the exact date of dismissal — that's when the 3-month clock starts</li>
  <li>Checked whether you were given 30 days' notice or 30 days' pay in lieu</li>
  <li>Filed with the Labor Relations Commission online, in person, by fax, or by mail — no fee</li>
  <li>Decided whether you want reinstatement or monetary compensation before filing</li>
  <li>Asked about free 공인노무사 representation if your average monthly wage is under 3,000,000 KRW</li>
  <li>Noted the 10-day and 15-day appeal deadlines in case either side disputes the ruling</li>
  <li>Saved your contract, any termination notice, and payslips as evidence</li>
</ul>
