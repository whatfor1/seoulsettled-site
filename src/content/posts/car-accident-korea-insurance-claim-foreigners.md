Our [car-buying guide](/buy-car-korea-foreigners-registration-insurance/) covers registering a car and getting compulsory insurance in place before you drive it. This one covers the part nobody wants to think about until it happens: what Korean law actually requires of you in the minutes after a crash, how much your insurance pays out and under what caps, and what happens if you're the one who gets hit by someone with no insurance at all.

## The two duties at the scene are separate

Under [Article 54 of the Road Traffic Act, as summarized on casenote.kr](https://casenote.kr/법령/도로교통법/제54조), a driver involved in an accident that injures someone or damages property must immediately stop and do two things: provide help to anyone hurt and take "necessary measures" (사상자를 구호하는 등 필요한 조치), and give the other party your name, phone number, and address. That's the duty to the other person.

The second duty is to the police. You must report the accident without delay — to an officer at the scene or to the nearest police station — covering where it happened, how many people were hurt and how badly, what property was damaged, and what you've already done about it. The one exception: if only a parked, unoccupied vehicle was damaged and you've already taken safety measures (like moving it out of traffic), you don't have to report it.

These two duties carry very different penalties if you skip them:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Leaving the scene without helping or exchanging information (violating the first duty) — up to 5 years in prison or a fine up to 15,000,000 won, under Article 148 of the Road Traffic Act</li>
  <li>Helping the injured but not reporting to police (violating the second duty only) — up to 300,000 won or detention, under Article 154</li>
</ul>

Both figures come from direct citations on [casenote.kr's Article 148 page](https://casenote.kr/법령/도로교통법/제148조) and [Article 154 page](https://casenote.kr/법령/도로교통법/제154조). The gap between the two penalties is the point: Korean law treats abandoning someone you've just hit as a completely different order of offense from forgetting the paperwork.

## If someone is actually hurt, fleeing is a much bigger charge

Article 54 above covers the basic reporting duty. If you cause an injury or death and then flee (도주) rather than just failing to report, a separate law — the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes — takes over, and the penalties jump sharply. [Article 5-3 of that Act, quoted on casenote.kr](https://casenote.kr/법령/특정범죄가중처벌등에관한법률/제5조의3), sets out four tiers depending on the outcome:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Fled after injuring someone, victim not moved: at least 1 year in prison, or a fine of 5,000,000–30,000,000 won</li>
  <li>Fled after injuring someone, then moved and abandoned the victim: at least 3 years in prison</li>
  <li>Fled after a death, victim not moved: life imprisonment or at least 5 years in prison</li>
  <li>Fled after a death, then moved and abandoned the victim: death penalty, life imprisonment, or at least 5 years in prison</li>
</ul>

Moving an injured person and leaving them somewhere else — rather than just driving away — is treated as a materially worse act under this law, not a technicality. There's no insurance exemption for any of this; having a policy affects compensation to the victim, not whether you can be prosecuted for fleeing.

## Having insurance usually stops a prosecution — except for 12 situations

For ordinary negligent-injury accidents (no fleeing involved), Korean law normally keeps the criminal process out of it once insurance or a settlement is in place. [Article 3 of the Act on Special Cases Concerning the Settlement of Traffic Accidents, via casenote.kr](https://casenote.kr/법령/교통사고처리특례법/제3조), sets the base penalty for causing injury through negligent driving at up to 5 years' imprisonment or a fine up to 20,000,000 won — but says prosecutors generally can't proceed without the victim's consent. [Article 4 of the same Act](https://casenote.kr/법령/교통사고처리특례법/제4조) extends that further: if your vehicle carries qualifying comprehensive insurance or mutual-aid coverage, prosecution is barred outright for a simple negligent-injury case, full stop.

That protection does not apply if any of these 12 factors are involved — in which case you can be prosecuted regardless of insurance or a settlement:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Running a red light or ignoring a police officer's signal</li>
  <li>Crossing the center line, or an illegal U-turn or reverse</li>
  <li>Speeding more than 20 km/h over the limit</li>
  <li>Illegal overtaking or merging</li>
  <li>Violating railroad crossing rules</li>
  <li>Failing to yield to a pedestrian in a crosswalk</li>
  <li>Driving without a valid license</li>
  <li>Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or refusing a sobriety test</li>
  <li>Driving on the sidewalk</li>
  <li>Failing to secure a passenger's safety (e.g. seatbelt-related violations tied to the accident)</li>
  <li>Causing injury in a designated child-protection zone</li>
  <li>Unsecured cargo causing the accident</li>
</ul>

The same Article 4 also removes the insurance-based exemption if the victim suffers a life-threatening injury, permanent disability, or an incurable illness, or if the insurance contract itself turns out to be void, cancelled, or excluded from paying. If your accident touches any of the 12 items above — speeding 20 over, for instance, which is easy to hit without noticing on a highway — insurance does not make the criminal exposure go away.

## What your compulsory insurance actually pays out

Every registered car in Korea carries compulsory liability insurance (책임보험) covering injury to other people. Per [easylaw.go.kr's summary of compulsory motor insurance](https://www.easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?csmSeq=684&ccfNo=4&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1), confirmed independently on the [Financial Services Commission's own site](https://fsc.go.kr/po010101/72895), the caps are:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Death: up to 150,000,000 won per victim</li>
  <li>Permanent disability: up to 150,000,000 won, scaled by a 1–14 disability grade</li>
  <li>Injury without permanent disability: up to 30,000,000 won, scaled by a 1–14 injury grade</li>
  <li>Property damage: up to 20,000,000 won per accident</li>
</ul>

These are liability caps, not guaranteed payouts — the amount you actually receive depends on medical costs, lost income, and the fault ratio between the drivers, assessed against these ceilings. Comprehensive insurance (종합보험), which most drivers carry on top of the compulsory minimum, raises these limits considerably and also covers your own vehicle and injuries, but the compulsory figures above are the floor every driver is legally required to carry.

## If the other driver has no insurance, or you can't find them

This is the scenario foreign drivers worry about most, and Korea has a specific safety net for it: the government guarantee program (정부보장사업), run by the Korea Transportation Safety Authority's Motor Vehicle Accident Compensation institute, [자동차손해배상진흥원 (tacss.or.kr)](https://tacss.or.kr/majorproject/gep_03.jsp). It pays victims of hit-and-run accidents (where the other driver can't be identified), accidents with an uninsured vehicle, and accidents involving a stolen or unauthorized vehicle whose owner isn't liable.

The caps mirror compulsory insurance: death pays 20,000,000–150,000,000 won depending on when the accident happened (the 150,000,000 won ceiling applies to accidents from April 2016 onward), injury pays up to 30,000,000 won by grade, and permanent disability pays up to 150,000,000 won by grade. This program only covers injury to people — it does not pay for vehicle or property damage. You have **3 years from when you became aware of the damage** (normally the accident date) to file a claim, through a regional branch of 자동차손해배상진흥원 or by calling their integrated hotline at **1544-0049**. Required documents include a claim form, medical records, and the police accident report, so filing the police report from the first section above isn't just a legal duty — it's also the paperwork this claim needs later.

## When fault is disputed

Insurers, not individual drivers, settle most fault disputes directly, but when two insurers can't agree on a fault ratio, either side's insurer can refer the case to the Fault Ratio Dispute Mediation Committee, administered through [KNIA's accident-fault portal (accident.knia.or.kr)](https://accident.knia.or.kr/system). You as the policyholder can't file with the committee directly — you have to ask your own insurer to refer it on your behalf. The process runs in up to three tiers (an initial representative-level conference, a small committee of one or two attorneys, and a four-attorney re-examination committee for further appeals), each with a 14-day window to object to that tier's decision before it becomes final. Average resolution time across all tiers combined was about 67 days in the most recent published data, though a case resolved at the first tier moves faster than one that goes all the way to re-examination.

## Tell your own insurer quickly, even if the other side is clearly at fault

Separately from the police report, you also have a duty to your own insurer. [Article 657 of the Commercial Act, via casenote.kr](https://casenote.kr/법령/상법/제657조), requires the policyholder, insured person, or beneficiary to notify the insurer "without delay" (지체없이) once they become aware of the accident. If you delay and the damage grows worse as a result, the law is explicit: "보험자는 그 증가된 손해를 보상할 책임이 없다" — the insurer has no obligation to cover the portion of the loss that increased because you waited. This doesn't erase your claim entirely, but it can shrink it, and it's an easy rule to not know as a newcomer who assumes a phone call can wait until tomorrow.

## When it goes wrong

**You left the scene without realizing you had to stop.** Report it to the nearest police station as soon as you realize, and be straightforward about the timeline. Article 148's penalty applies to not stopping or not providing aid/information — showing up late to report on your own initiative, before anyone forces you to, is a materially different position than being tracked down.

**The other driver has no insurance and you can't get them to pay.** Don't wait on them — file directly with 자동차손해배상진흥원 at 1544-0049 within the 3-year window. The program exists precisely so you aren't dependent on an uninsured driver's ability or willingness to pay.

**Your insurer is lowballing you, or you disagree with the fault split.** Ask your insurer in writing to refer the case to the Fault Ratio Dispute Mediation Committee rather than accepting the first number offered — you can't file with the committee yourself, but you can request that your insurer do it.

**You're not sure whether your accident falls under one of the 12 major-fault exceptions.** Assume it might rather than assuming it doesn't — items like "20 km/h over the limit" or a child-protection-zone injury are easy to trigger without a dramatic sense of wrongdoing, and they remove the usual insurance-based protection from prosecution.

## Checklist

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Stopped, checked for injuries, and exchanged name/phone/address with the other driver</li>
  <li>Reported to police unless it was only parked-vehicle damage with safety measures already taken</li>
  <li>Notified my own insurer without delay, not after a few days</li>
  <li>Kept the police accident report — needed for insurance claims and for the government guarantee program if the other side turns out uninsured</li>
  <li>If the other driver is unidentified or uninsured: contacted 자동차손해배상진흥원 (1544-0049) well inside the 3-year deadline</li>
  <li>Checked whether any of the 12 major-fault factors apply before assuming insurance covers everything</li>
</ul>
