Getting a Korean phone number is a chicken-and-egg problem: you need a number for banking apps and delivery, but the best plans need a residence card you don't have yet. Here is how to get a Korean phone number as a foreigner, in the right order, what it actually costs at each stage, and when prepaid beats postpaid.

## The fast path

On arrival, buy a prepaid plan with just your passport — a KT prepaid line starts at a 10,000 won top-up that stays active for 60 days, so you're not locked into anything. Once your residence card (ARC) arrives, switch to a monthly postpaid or budget (MVNO) plan; moving carriers while keeping your number costs a flat 800 won (+VAT) MNP fee, nothing more. Don't buy anything expensive on day one.

## Prepaid vs postpaid in Korea: the real difference

Korea has three mobile carriers: SK Telecom, KT and LG U+. According to the government's [Korea.net guide to getting mobile service](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Society/view?articleId=169210), a passport lets you sign up only for a prepaid plan. A monthly plan needs an ARC, and you might also need a Korean bank account or credit card. Requirements differ by carrier.

In plain terms:

- **Prepaid (선불):** you pay first, then use. Passport only. Fast, but it is meant for the first weeks.

- **Postpaid (후불):** a monthly bill, usually auto-debited from a Korean account or card. Needs your ARC.

- **MVNO (알뜰폰):** budget brands that rent the big three's networks, often far cheaper than the big three's own plans. Many need an ARC too, so check before you walk in.

## Step 1: Get a prepaid plan with your passport

Walk into a carrier store or pick up a SIM at the airport, show your passport, and pick a plan. In the Korea.net example the clerk finishes it in minutes. Choose the cheap one — you will replace it soon.

KT's own [prepaid price page](https://shop.kt.com/m/smart/phonePricePopView.do?prodCtgCd=11&usimType=LTE) lays out charging tiers with no sign-up fee, no base rate and no contract: top up 10,000 won and the line stays active 60 days; 30,000 won covers 180 days plus 100 minutes of KT-to-KT calls; 50,000 won covers a full 365 days plus 200 minutes. Everything above that is pay-as-you-go, charged in small increments per second of calls and per text.

If you'd rather have real data from day one, SK Telecom's budget arm SK 7mobile [publishes its line-up](https://www.sk7mobile.com/prod/data/callingPlanList.do?refCode=USIM): 1,900 won a month for 500MB and 60 minutes, up to 16,900 won for 7GB with unlimited throttled data, or 33,000 won for 11GB with a faster 3Mbps throttle. These are self-service MVNO plans, not the KT-style pay-as-you-go prepaid above, but they show how wide the price range actually is before you've even seen an ARC-only postpaid plan.

**Jun's tip:** a passport-registered prepaid number may not pass the identity checks that banks and some apps run. Treat it as a bridge for maps, messaging and calls, and don't be surprised if a verification code fails.

## Step 2: Register your ARC, then bank

Most people wait a few weeks for the card. If you haven't started, read [how to get your ARC in Korea](/how-to-get-arc-korea/). Then, because many postpaid plans bill by auto-debit, read [how to open a Korean bank account as a foreigner](/open-bank-account-korea-foreigner/).

## Step 3: Switch to postpaid or an MVNO

Bring your ARC, passport and your account or card details to a store. The Korea.net clerk also warns that the plan you qualify for can depend on your visa type, so ask what your visa allows.

Switching carriers later doesn't mean losing your number. Number portability (번호이동, MNP) between SK Telecom, KT and LG U+ costs a flat 800 won plus VAT, confirmed on [KT's own account-guide page](https://help.kt.com/serviceinfo/ServiceJoinGuideL2.do) — there's no separate penalty for the move itself, only for breaking a postpaid contract early (more on that below).

You don't have to figure out the Korean on your own. KT's [customer center page](https://help.kt.com/store/KtCustCenter.do) lists a foreigner line at 080-448-0100 (free) or 02-2190-1180 (paid), plus +82-2-2190-0901 if you're calling from overseas; general questions run weekdays 09:00–18:00 with a lunch break from 12:00–13:00, but lost-phone, suspension and call-quality issues are handled 24 hours. SK Telecom's general line is 080-011-6000 and LG U+'s is 1544-0010 — confirm English support is available before you call, since the carriers don't guarantee it on every line. The [Seoul Global Center](https://global.seoul.go.kr/hmpg/main/main.do) also helps with this kind of errand in person or by phone at 02-2075-4180.

**Jun's tip:** ask for the foreigner-support staff by saying "English, please" at the counter, or book a visit time through the carrier's English line. A store at a busy hour is the worst place to compare plans.

## When something goes wrong

**A bank or delivery app won't verify your number.** This usually means the line is still registered as a passport-only prepaid SIM rather than one tied to your real-name ARC record. Move to a postpaid or MVNO line once your ARC arrives; most of these verification failures clear up the moment the carrier's registration matches your ARC details.

**The store asks for a deposit before approving postpaid.** Carriers can ask foreign residents without a Korean credit history for a security deposit before issuing a postpaid line. The amount isn't fixed anywhere official I could find and varies by carrier, branch and your visa, so ask the specific store for its own policy rather than trusting a number from the internet.

**You lose your phone or SIM.** Call your carrier's suspension line immediately to freeze the line against unauthorized use — KT's lost-phone and suspension line runs 24 hours, even outside the 09:00–18:00 window for general questions.

**You want to cancel a postpaid contract early.** The MNP fee itself is only 800 won (+VAT), but if your plan came with a device discount or subsidy, leaving before the contract term ends usually means paying back part of that subsidy as an early-termination charge. The amount depends entirely on what you were given at sign-up, so ask the store for the exact payout schedule before you sign, not after.

**Your visa type doesn't qualify for the plan you wanted.** Some postpaid and MVNO plans are restricted by visa category. If you're turned down, ask the clerk which plans your specific visa does qualify for instead of assuming prepaid is your only option.

## Which should you pick: prepaid or postpaid?

If you are staying a few weeks, stay on prepaid. It needs only a passport, and you avoid contracts and bank forms. If you are staying a year or more, treat prepaid as a short bridge and plan the switch the day your ARC arrives.

The question that decides it is simple: do you need the number to work for banking, delivery apps and government sites? If yes, you want a line tied to your ARC. If you just need maps and messages, a passport line is fine.

A small habit helps: write down the date your ARC is expected, and book your carrier visit for the same week. That way your bridge plan never quietly rolls over into another expensive top-up.

## How to compare plans without being overwhelmed

Prices change often, so treat the figures above as a starting point, not a quote. Compare these instead:

1. **Monthly data and what happens when it runs out.** Some plans slow down rather than stop — KT's prepaid tiers above are pay-as-you-go instead, so check which model you're actually buying.

2. **Contract length.** A shorter commitment is safer if your visa is short, since breaking a long contract early can mean repaying a device subsidy.

3. **Payment method.** Make sure your foreign credit card or Korean account is accepted.

4. **Support language.** Cheaper MVNOs often have thinner English support.

5. **What happens if you leave.** Ask about cancelling when your visa ends, and what the early-termination math looks like in won, not just in principle.

## Common mistakes

**Buying a long prepaid plan.** You'll switch within weeks, so top up small — a 10,000 won KT top-up only buys 60 days anyway.

**Assuming your ARC isn't needed.** Without it you're limited to prepaid.

**Skipping the identity step.** If an app won't verify your number, it may be a passport-registered line. Move to an ARC-linked number. Our [identity verification guide](/korea-identity-verification-pass-app-foreigners/) covers why PASS, Kakao인증 and the financial certificate all fail the same way on a passport-only line.

**Not asking about your visa.** The Korea.net clerk says plan eligibility can differ by visa type, so ask before choosing.

**Assuming switching carriers is free.** The 800 won MNP fee is trivial, but an early-termination charge on a subsidized postpaid plan is not — ask before you cancel.
