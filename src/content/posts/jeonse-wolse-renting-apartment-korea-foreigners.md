You found a nice place in Seoul, and the listing says "전세" or "월세" with a deposit that looks like a typo. Nobody warned you that renting an apartment in Korea works nothing like renting back home. Here is how jeonse and wolse work, and what to check before you hand over any money.

## Short version

Jeonse means a huge lump-sum deposit and no monthly rent. Wolse means a smaller deposit plus rent every month. Whichever you choose, the paperwork after signing protects your deposit more than the contract does.

## Jeonse and wolse: what is the difference?

The Seoul Metropolitan Government [explains both on its housing page](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/living/housing/1-wolse-jeonse/). With jeonse, the tenant gives the landlord a large deposit and leases the home for 1 to 2 years. Ten percent is paid when you sign, and the rest when you move in. At the end, the landlord must return the full amount.

With wolse, you pay a smaller deposit and a monthly fee. The same page says the deposit is generally ten to twenty times the monthly rent. Again, about ten percent is paid as the contract fee and the rest on move-in.

Two details from that page matter. If a tenant ends a wolse contract midway, they are not eligible for a deposit refund. If the landlord ends it midway, the landlord must compensate the tenant with double the deposit. Unpaid rent or utilities can also be deducted from what you get back.

Jun’s tip: when you compare listings, do the math on the deposit as money you cannot use. A wolse with a larger deposit and lower rent is not “cheaper” if the deposit has to come out of savings you need.

## Who is protected by Korean housing law?

This is the part most guides skip. Korea’s [easy law portal](https://m.easylaw.go.kr/MOM/SubCsmOvRetrieve.laf?ccfNo=1&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1&csmSeq=2495&langCd=700101) says the Housing Lease Protection Act is intended to protect Korean nationals. In principle, foreigners are not covered. However, a foreigner who has completed alien registration and reported their change of residence may be exceptionally protected.

That is why your ARC and your address report are not just admin chores. If you have not done the first one yet, start with our guide to [getting your ARC in Korea](/how-to-get-arc-korea/).

## Before you sign: what to check

The same portal, citing the HUG jeonse fraud prevention site, lists the key checks before signing:

- Make sure the deposit is appropriate relative to the market price of the home.

- Check the certified copy of the real estate register for the owner, mortgage holders and existing tenants.

- Check the building register to see whether the property is unauthorized or illegally built.

- Confirm that the person signing as landlord matches the owner on the register. If an agent signs, check their ID, the power of attorney with the owner’s seal, and the seal certificate.

- Check whether the landlord has overdue national or local taxes.

- Confirm that the realtor is licensed.

- Write any verbal promises into the special terms section of the contract.

On signing day, before the final payment, look at the register again to confirm nothing changed and the previous tenant has moved out.

Jun’s tip: ask the agent to send you the register copy and the contract text a day early, then run both through a translation app. A rushed reading at the agency desk is where mistakes happen.

## After you sign: the three steps that protect your deposit

According to the easy law portal, do these after signing:

1. Move in and file a change of residence report within 15 days. This secures your right to stay and to recover the deposit if ownership changes.

2. Obtain a fixed date (확정일자) on the contract. This gives you priority over later creditors if the home is auctioned or sold.

3. If the deposit is over KRW 60 million or the monthly rent is over KRW 300,000, report the lease within 30 days. Submitting the contract is deemed an application for the fixed date.

You can report a change of residence at immigration, a local administrative welfare center, or through [HiKorea](https://www.hikorea.go.kr/). A fixed date can be obtained at the administrative welfare center, district court or registry office, or through the Supreme Court’s Internet Registry Office.

## Deposit guarantee insurance and where to get help

For extra protection, the portal suggests a financial guarantee product that covers the refund of your deposit. It lists the Korea Housing & Urban Guarantee Corporation (HUG), the Korea Housing Finance Corporation (HF) and Seoul Guarantee Insurance (SGI). Eligibility differs by provider, so contact them directly.

If you need advice in English, the Seoul Foreign Resident Center offers real estate consulting on Mondays from 2 PM to 5 PM at 40 Dosin-ro, Yeongdeungpo-gu. You can walk in, call 02-2229-4900 or email help@sfrc.seoul.kr, per the Seoul housing page above. The same page points to a list of real estate agencies that handle foreign languages.
