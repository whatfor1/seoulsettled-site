Having a baby in Korea and wondering whether you get paid leave for it? The honest answer is: it depends on a step most guides skip entirely. Maternity leave pay (출산전후휴가급여) and parental leave pay (육아휴직급여) are not employer benefits — they're paid out of the Employment Insurance (고용보험) fund, the same fund that pays unemployment benefits. If you're not enrolled in Employment Insurance, there's no pot to draw from, no matter how long you've worked or how large your company is.

This guide covers both benefits for foreign workers specifically: who's actually enrolled by visa type, the leave lengths and payout caps for 2026, how the money is split between your employer and the government, and what to do if your employer refuses leave or the Employment Insurance office says you're not on file. Every figure below is checked against Korea's Enforcement Decree of the Employment Insurance Act (고용보험법 시행령), the Ministry of Employment and Labor's own announcements, and [Gov.kr's official service page](https://www.gov.kr/portal/service/serviceInfo/WII000001460), current as of November 26, 2026.

## Step one: are you even enrolled in Employment Insurance?

This is the gate everything else sits behind, and it runs by visa, not by job title.

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>F-2 (residence, including the points-based F-2-7 track), F-5 (permanent residency), and F-6 (marriage migrant) holders are mandatory Employment Insurance subscribers — enrolled automatically from the hire date, same as a Korean national</li>
  <li>E-1 through E-7, E-9, E-10, C-4, F-4, and H-2 holders fall under voluntary coverage (임의가입) — your employer has to agree to enroll you, and it doesn't happen automatically just because you're on payroll</li>
  <li>The [official EPS (고용허가제) guidance for E-9 workers](https://www.eps.go.kr/eo/EmployPerSystem.eo?tabGb=06) confirms this directly: Employment Insurance is 임의가입 for E-9, unlike industrial accident insurance and health insurance, which are mandatory for the same visa</li>
</ul>

If you're on a voluntary-enrollment visa, check your actual 4대보험 enrollment status before assuming either benefit applies to you — a payslip deduction for health insurance doesn't mean you're also in the Employment Insurance system. Ask payroll directly, or check through the National Pension Service's integrated 4대보험 portal. I covered this same enrollment split in more detail in the [unemployment benefits guide](/unemployment-benefits-korea-foreign-workers/) — the visa categories are identical because both benefits draw from the same Employment Insurance fund.

## Maternity leave pay (출산전후휴가급여)

Under Article 74 of the Labor Standards Act (근로기준법), a pregnant employee gets 90 days of leave spanning before and after birth — 100 days for a premature birth, 120 days for twins or more. At least 45 days (60 for multiples) has to fall after the birth; the remainder can be used before.

**Who pays which part** depends on company size:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>At a 우선지원대상기업 (a smaller company meeting the government's size thresholds for priority support), Employment Insurance pays the full 90 days directly</li>
  <li>At a larger company, the employer pays the first 60 days (75 for multiples) at full ordinary wage, and Employment Insurance pays only the remaining 30 days (45 for multiples), capped</li>
</ul>

**The 2026 cap:** [the Ministry of Employment and Labor's maternity-leave-pay ceiling for 2026](https://www.moel.go.kr/news/enews/report/enewsView.do?news_seq=18514) is 2.2 million won on a 30-day basis — up from 2.1 million won. That's the ceiling on what Employment Insurance itself pays per 30-day stretch; it doesn't cap what a large employer owes you directly for the first 60 days, since that portion is paid at your full ordinary wage by the company, not by the fund.

**Eligibility:** you need a cumulative 180 days of insured employment before your leave ends. **Apply** at your local employment center (고용센터), online through Work24 (work24.go.kr), or by mail — starting one month after your leave begins and up to 12 months after it ends. Miss the 12-month window and the claim is gone.

## Parental leave (육아휴직) — the time off itself

Separately from maternity leave, either parent can take up to one year of 육아휴직 per child, usable until the child turns 8 or finishes second grade, whichever comes first. You need 180 days of insured employment before the leave starts, and your employer can't refuse a request that meets the legal conditions — though in practice, enforcement runs through the same complaint channel covered below.

One condition matters for dual-income households: if you want the enhanced "6+6" payout described next, your spouse also has to be taking leave for the same child, concurrently.

## Parental leave pay (육아휴직급여) — the 2026 numbers

This is where the 2026 changes are large. Under the amended [Enforcement Decree of the Employment Insurance Act (Article 95, Presidential Decree No. 36588)](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1000954744&chrClsCd=010202), effective since September 18, 2026, the monthly cap now steps down in three tiers instead of being flat:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Months 1-3: up to 2.5 million won/month (floor 700,000 won) — up from a flat 1.5 million won before the 2026 revision</li>
  <li>Months 4-6: up to 2.0 million won/month (floor 700,000 won)</li>
  <li>Month 7 onward: 80% of ordinary wage, up to 1.6 million won/month (floor 700,000 won)</li>
</ul>

The other change that matters as much as the amount: the old 25%-withheld-until-you-return-to-work-for-6-months rule (사후지급금) is gone for leave taken under the new structure. You get the full monthly amount during the leave itself, not a partial payment followed by a holdback.

**Eligibility and application** mirror maternity pay: 180 days insured before the leave starts, 30+ consecutive days of leave taken, apply from one month after the leave starts to 12 months after it ends, with your employer's leave-confirmation document attached to the claim.

## If both parents take leave: the "6+6" bonus

If you and your spouse both take 육아휴직 for a child under 18 months, concurrently, each parent's payout gets a temporary boost for the first six months instead of the standard three-tier schedule. The Ministry of Employment and Labor's 2026 announcement raised the first month's cap under this track from 2.0 million won to 2.5 million won, with the monthly cap continuing to climb through the six-month window toward a peak well above the standard tiers. Because the exact month-by-month cap depends on each parent's ordinary wage and which month they're on, don't calculate your own total from a blog table — ask your 고용센터 for the figure that applies to your actual pay before you plan around it.

## A separate benefit: the spouse's leave

If you're the partner rather than the person giving birth, there's a third, distinct benefit: 배우자 출산전후휴가 (spouse's maternity-period leave). As of [the law's September 18, 2026 revision](https://www.moel.go.kr/news/enews/report/enewsView.do?news_seq=19768), this is 20 days, fully paid, usable anytime from 50 days before the due date to 120 days after birth, and splittable. This comes from your employer, not Employment Insurance, and it's separate from both benefits above — the paperwork is just a document stating the expected or actual birth date, which your employer uses to grant the 20 days within the eligible window.

## If something goes wrong

<ul class="checklist">
  <li><strong>Employer refuses or blocks the leave outright:</strong> refusing legally-required maternity leave under Article 74 is a criminal violation of the Labor Standards Act, carrying up to 2 years' imprisonment or a fine of up to 20 million won [under Article 110](https://www.law.go.kr/lsLinkCommonInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1014811973). Report it to your local 지방고용노동청 or call the labor ministry's 1350 counseling line before assuming you have no recourse</li>
  <li><strong>Employment center says you're not enrolled in Employment Insurance:</strong> if your employer should have enrolled you and simply didn't, retroactive enrollment can be requested — bring your employment contract and pay records to the 고용센터 and ask specifically about retroactive (소급) enrollment rather than accepting "not enrolled" as final</li>
  <li><strong>Your benefit claim gets rejected:</strong> you can file an objection (심사청구) with an Employment Insurance examiner, and if that's denied, a further appeal (재심사청구) to the Employment Insurance Examination Committee — both channels exist specifically because first-pass rejections happen and aren't automatically final</li>
  <li><strong>Sources disagree on your specific case:</strong> visa-by-visa exceptions and company-size thresholds for 우선지원대상기업 status have edge cases a general guide can't cover. Call 1350 or visit your local 고용센터 with your actual contract and payslips before assuming either way</li>
</ul>

## Checklist before you apply

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Confirm your actual Employment Insurance enrollment status — don't assume from your visa type or your payslip deductions alone</li>
  <li>Count your insured days: you need 180 days before the leave starts (parental) or ends (maternity)</li>
  <li>Get your employer's leave-confirmation document before you apply — the claim won't process without it</li>
  <li>Mark the 12-month deadline after your leave ends; after that, the claim is gone regardless of eligibility</li>
  <li>If you're a dual-income household considering concurrent leave, ask your 고용센터 for your specific "6+6" monthly figures before relying on a generic table</li>
</ul>

## What's next

Employment Insurance eligibility questions don't stop at parental leave — the same 180-day rule and visa-based enrollment split determine [unemployment benefits](/unemployment-benefits-korea-foreign-workers/) if you later lose that job. Severance pay runs on completely separate rules and comes from your employer directly, covered in the [severance pay guide](/korea-severance-pay-foreign-workers/). If your employer withholds any of these payments after you're owed them, that becomes an [unpaid wages case](/unpaid-wages-korea-foreign-workers/), and if you were injured on the job rather than on leave, that's [workplace injury insurance](/workplace-injury-insurance-korea-foreign-workers/) territory instead. Whichever benefit lands in your account, it needs somewhere to go — see the [bank account guide](/open-bank-account-korea-foreigner/) if you haven't set one up yet.
