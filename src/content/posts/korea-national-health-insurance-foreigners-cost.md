Your first Korean paycheck arrives smaller than the contract said, or a bill for health insurance lands in your mailbox and you have no idea why. Korea health insurance for foreigners is mandatory for most residents, and the amount depends on one thing: whether you are on a company payroll or not. This guide explains both cases with the 2026 numbers.

## Short version

If you work for a Korean company, your employer enrolls you and takes your share from your salary. If you live here without a Korean employer, you are generally enrolled after 6 months of stay, and you get a monthly bill. Either way, the coverage is the same as Koreans get.

## Who has to join Korea’s national health insurance

The National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) [guidance for foreigners](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do) splits people into two groups.

**Employees.** Foreigners who work at an insured company are compulsorily subscribed. NHIS says you can apply for exclusion if you can prove equivalent medical coverage under foreign regulations or insurance, or through a contract with your employer. You file the withdrawal form within 14 days of the date your eligibility was reported.

**Everyone else (regional subscribers).** NHIS states that any foreigner or overseas Korean who has stayed in Korea for over 6 months is subject to mandatory subscription, a rule in place since July 16, 2019. The visa list on that page covers D, E, F and H categories, including students on D-2 and D-4.

## Adding a dependent without a second premium

If you're an employee subscriber, NHIS lets you add family members as dependents for free — no separate premium — as long as they clear its income and property tests. The same [guidance for foreigners page](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02900m01.do) sets the bar: total annual income (business, finance, pension, employment and other income combined) under 20,000,000 won, and no more than 5,000,000 won in business income if they're registered as self-employed. On the property side, their property tax base has to stay under 540,000,000 won, or under 10,000,000 won in annual income if it's between 540,000,000 won and 900,000,000 won. Siblings face a tighter 180,000,000 won property cap and must also be over 65 years old, under 30 years old, disabled, or otherwise exempt from the usual age limit. Spouses, parents, children and their spouses qualify automatically; unmarried siblings need to clear the extra conditions above.

## How much you pay if you have a job

For employees, NHIS sets the [2026 contribution rate](https://www.nhis.or.kr/english/wbheaa02500m01.do) at 7.19% of your monthly wage. The company and the employee each pay 50%, and your share is deducted from your salary. The rate was 7.09% in 2025, so a small increase hit every payslip in January. Whichever group you're in, NHIS fixes the due date at the 10th of the following month.

NHIS’s [2026 rate notice](https://www.nhis.or.kr/nhis/etc/20251204_banner_pop01.do) (in Korean) also lists long-term care insurance at 0.9448%, calculated as a share of your health premium. Those two lines together are why your payslip shows more than one insurance deduction.

**Jun’s tip:** Read your payslip once, line by line, in your first month. Health insurance, long-term care, the pension and employment insurance are separate items, and knowing which is which makes it easy to spot a real mistake.

## How much you pay if you don’t have a Korean employer

Regional subscribers pay by a points system based on income, property and cars. The NHIS contribution page sets the value per point at 211.5 won for 2026. Property includes things like a rented home, so your housing situation matters. There is also a floor. The NHIS guidance page says that if the calculated fee is less than the average fee, the average fee is charged. The 2026 notice puts the average premium for foreign regional subscribers at 158,630 won a month, made up of 140,210 won in health insurance and 18,420 won in long-term care. Holders of F-5 and F-6 visas are the exception: NHIS says their fee is calculated the same way as for Korean residents.

## Student reductions and other discounts

NHIS’s foreigner page lists a reduction for D-2 and D-4 visa holders, and for overseas Korean students on F-4, of 50% from March 2023 onward, except for households made up only of minors. It also lists 50% for people living on islands or in remote areas, and 22% for people living in farming or fishing villages. Check the page for your exact case, because the rules differ by visa.

## What the insurance covers

NHIS says insurance for foreigners has the same coverage as for Korean citizens: prevention, diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation of illness or injury, plus childbirth, death and health promotion. In practice, that is why a clinic visit costs so little once you show your card, and why the pharmacist next door only charges you the co-pay. ## If something goes wrong

**You missed the 14-day exclusion window.** NHIS's guidance only describes the Employee Health Insurance Withdrawal Application filed within 14 days of your eligibility date — it doesn't list a late option. If you're past that window, call the English line at 033-811-2000 and ask NHIS directly whether an exception applies to your case before assuming you're stuck paying for two policies at once.

**A bill never arrived because your address was out of date.** NHIS still expects payment. Its contribution-rate page states that if the insured fails to pay, the Service "may take an action to collect it by authority, according to the law." A missing bill isn't the same as no debt — call 1577-1000 to check your balance before it builds up.

**Your dependent application was rejected.** If a family member's income or property is over the limits above, NHIS enrolls them separately as a regional subscriber, billed by the points system, instead of riding free on your employee policy.

**Your employer never reported your enrollment.** The foreigner guidance page puts that reporting duty on the employer, not you. If nothing shows up as a deduction on your first payslip, ask HR to confirm the report was filed — don't wait for a surprise regional-subscriber bill to find out it wasn't.

## Step by step: what to do this week

1. Check which group you are in: employee or regional subscriber.

2. If you are an employee, confirm with your HR team that enrollment was reported, and look at the deduction on your payslip.

3. If you are a regional subscriber, expect your first bill after the 6-month point, and set up automatic payment so you never miss a month.

4. If you have equivalent coverage from your home country or employer and think you qualify for exclusion, ask NHIS before the filing window closes, since the employee form has a 14-day limit.

5. Save the NHIS call center number: 1577-1000, or the English line at 033-811-2000.
