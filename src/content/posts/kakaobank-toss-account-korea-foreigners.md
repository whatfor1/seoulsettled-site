Until Kakaobank's nationwide foreigner push on October 12, 2026, opening a Korean bank account without a branch visit was mostly theory — one bank's app, with a long list of conditions attached. Kakaobank's own event framed the new push around roughly 2.2 million foreign residents in Korea, about 50 services already live in the app, and support in English, Chinese and Vietnamese now, with more languages planned for next year. Toss Bank has quietly run a similar app-only path for a while. Neither replaces the branch-based account covered in the [bank account guide](/open-bank-account-korea-foreigner/) — but if your only blocker is finding 30 minutes to sit at a branch counter, these are the two that currently skip it.

## Who actually qualifies

Kakaobank's own [foreigner account page](https://www.kakaobank.com/products/foreignWithdrawal) sets the bar: you need to be 19 years old or older, a Korean resident, and hold a Korean phone number registered in your own name. Acceptable ID is an Alien Registration Card, a Korean driver's license, a permanent residency card, or a domestic residence report — a passport by itself is explicitly not enough. You also need a Korean address on file, and the account cap is 2 per person.

Toss Bank's own [onboarding guide](https://toss.im/tossfeed/article/korealifehacks-7) accepts the ARC, the permanent residency card or the domestic residence report, plus a smartphone. A Korean phone number makes signup faster, but if you don't have one yet, an NFC-enabled electronic passport can stand in for phone-based verification instead — useful in the gap between landing and getting a SIM.

## Opening a Toss Bank account

The process runs in two stages. First, download the Toss app and enter your name, contact details and ID information, then complete identity verification. Once that clears, tap "계좌 만들기" (create account) on the home screen to actually open the Toss Bank account — the app and the bank account are separate steps, and skipping straight to the second one without finishing the first just loops you back.

Once it's open, a foreign resident gets the same savings and term-deposit products, debit card, transfers and ATM withdrawals as a Korean national holder. Two things don't carry over: unsecured loan products — credit loans and the 마이너스통장 overdraft line — aren't available to foreign account holders at all, and a handful of sanctioned countries (Iran and North Korea among them) are excluded by nationality regardless of visa status.

A freshly opened Toss account starts capped at 300,000 KRW in transfers a day until your identity is fully verified. You can raise that to 50,000,000 KRW a day by photographing and submitting proof documents inside the app, a review that Toss says takes roughly 2 to 3 business days. If a rent deposit or a first paycheck is landing soon, submit those documents the same day you open the account rather than waiting until the transfer actually fails.

## Opening a Kakaobank account

Kakaobank's [own blog post on the process](https://blog.kakaobank.com/posts/foreign-account-opening) walks through a chatbot-driven flow: you photograph your ID, then verify your identity through one of three paths — a selfie match, a video call, or verification through an existing Korean bank account. The whole thing doesn't have to finish in one sitting; Kakaobank lets you pause and pick it back up within 7 days before the session expires and you have to restart. If you paid tax in another country within the past year, expect to be asked for extra documents — the exact list depends on nationality and is spelled out in an in-app "foreigner account checklist" rather than on the public site.

The account itself pays a base interest rate of 0.10% a year before tax, confirmed current on Kakaobank's product page as of this post's publish date. What's actually worth more than the interest: Kakaobank waives the inter-bank transfer fee, the inter-bank auto-transfer fee, every ATM fee at bank machines and convenience-store machines alike, and the certificate-issuance fee. Like every deposit account in Korea, it's covered by Korea Deposit Insurance up to 100,000,000 KRW per person, principal and interest combined — the same protection ceiling that applies to the branch accounts in our other banking guides.

## Sending money through these apps

Kakaobank's [remittance page](https://www.kakaobank.com/products/foreignRemittanceSend) lists two separate routes. The account-transfer route (해외계좌송금) costs 4,900 KRW for a won-denominated transfer, or 4,900 KRW plus 0.5% of the amount for a foreign-currency transfer, reaches about 22 countries, and typically lands in 1 to 3 days. The Western Union route (WU빠른해외송금) costs $5 USD, or $5 USD plus 1.0% of the amount, reaches roughly 200 countries, clears in about 1 minute, and caps at $7,000 a day.

What the page doesn't make clear is whether its $100,000 annual no-documentation figure applies to foreign residents specifically or to Korean-national customers generally — and that ambiguity matters, because our [separate guide to sending money abroad](/send-money-abroad-korea-foreigners/) found, through the Korea Federation of Banks' own page, that foreign residents are capped at $50,000 a year without documentation, with the new $100,000 allowance explicitly carved out for everyone except foreign residents. Rather than guess which figure applies to you, use Kakaobank or Toss for the fee savings on smaller transfers and check the dedicated remittance guide before assuming you have $100,000 of headroom.

## If something goes wrong

**Your account login stops working.** Kakaobank blocks app login 30 days after your registered stay or visa expiry date, and every identity-verification-dependent function — transfers, new cards, remittance — stops working once your stay has technically ended, even if you're mid-extension. Renew or extend your stay before that 30-day window closes, not after.

**Your application is rejected over a name mismatch.** Both apps check your name against your phone carrier, your ID and, for Kakaobank's bank-linked verification path, an existing account — character for character, spacing included. A mismatch anywhere in that chain fails the whole signup. Our [identity verification guide](/korea-identity-verification-pass-app-foreigners/) covers which system is actually running the check and how to fix the spelling at the source rather than retrying the same broken application.

**Your Toss transfer limit won't budge past 300,000 KRW a day.** That's the unverified-account default, not a system error. Submit the proof documents in-app; Toss says the review runs 2 to 3 business days, so don't wait until the night before a deadline to start it.

**You're applying for a credit product and it's declined.** Neither app issues unsecured credit to foreign account holders regardless of how long the account's been open — that's a Toss policy, not a rejection based on your file. If you need a credit card rather than a debit card, our [credit card guide](/credit-card-korea-foreigners/) covers which Korean banks actually extend credit to foreign residents and what gets an application approved.

## Checklist before you download either app

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Alien Registration Card, permanent residency card, or domestic residence report (passport alone isn't enough for Kakaobank)</li>
  <li>Korean phone number in your own name, or an NFC-enabled e-passport as a Toss fallback</li>
  <li>Age 19 or older, Korean address on file</li>
  <li>For Kakaobank: tax documents ready if you paid tax abroad in the past year</li>
  <li>For Toss: proof documents ready to raise the 300,000 KRW daily cap before you need the full 50,000,000 KRW limit</li>
  <li>A calendar reminder 30 days before your visa expires, so Kakaobank login doesn't lock you out</li>
</ul>
