On an E-9 (non-professional employment) visa, you don't automatically keep your legal status if you leave your employer — you get **1 month to apply** for a new workplace, and then up to **3 months** for immigration to actually approve the transfer. Miss both and the law says you have to leave Korea. None of this is a company policy; it's written directly into the Act on Employment, etc. of Foreign Workers (외국인근로자의 고용 등에 관한 법률), Article 25, and it applies whether you're changing because your employer wronged you or because your contract simply ran its course.

## Who this covers

This article is about E-9 workers — the employment permit (EPS) visa used in manufacturing, construction, agriculture, livestock, and fishing. H-2 (working visit) visa holders from Chinese or CIS ethnic-Korean backgrounds operate under a related but separate framework; some of the numbers below (the deadlines, the Article 25 text) are E-9-specific. If you're on E-7, D-10, or another work visa, your situation is a straightforward change of workplace through HiKOREA rather than the EPS job-center process described here — see my [visa status change guide](/change-visa-status-korea-foreigners/) instead.

## The reasons the law recognizes

Under [제25조 제1항](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=2042&ccfNo=3&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1), a workplace change has to fall into one of three categories:

- **Employer-side termination with cause** — your employer justifiably ends or refuses to renew your contract.
- **Employer fault, officially listed** — business suspension or closure, the employer's hiring permit being cancelled, an employment restriction imposed on the employer, an illegal dormitory, or the employer violating your working conditions or mistreating you. The Ministry of Employment and Labor maintains the specific list by public notice (고시).
- **Other grounds set by presidential decree** — a narrower catch-all for situations the other two don't cover.

The Ministry expanded the second category on April 1, 2021, after reports of workers stuck in abusive situations with no clean way out. The grounds added, confirmed on [korea.kr's policy briefing](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148891980), are specific enough to check against your own situation:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Your dormitory is a vinyl greenhouse or an illegal structure under the Building Act or Farmland Act</li>
  <li>A workplace safety violation left you needing 3 or more months of treatment for a physical or mental injury</li>
  <li>Sexual assault by a coworker, your employer's spouse, or their direct relatives — not just the employer themselves — qualifies for an emergency change</li>
  <li>Your employer refused to let you work for 5 or more consecutive days while withholding wages or the legally required 휴업수당 (shutdown allowance)</li>
</ul>

## The two deadlines that actually matter

This is where people lose status without realizing it. [제25조 제3항](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=2042&ccfNo=3&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1) sets two separate clocks, not one:

- **1 month** from the day your contract with your employer ends, to file a 사업장 변경신청서 (workplace-change application) with the head of your local Employment Center (고용센터의 장).
- **3 months** from the day you file that application, to actually receive the workplace-transfer permit from Immigration under [Article 21 of the Immigration Act](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/출입국관리법/제21조).

Miss either one and the statute says you must leave the country. There's one built-in exception: if a work injury, illness, pregnancy, or childbirth is what's stopping you from applying or from receiving the permit, both clocks pause and restart counting from the day that condition ends — you don't lose the time you couldn't use.

## How many times you can switch

You don't get unlimited changes. [제25조 제4항](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=2042&ccfNo=3&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1) caps it at **3 times** during your initial work-permitted period and **2 times** during the extended period under the re-employment special case (재고용 특례). The [Ministry's own explainer](https://www.moel.go.kr/policyitrd/policyItrdView.do?policy_itrd_sn=42) frames those same limits against the actual calendar: 3 changes across your first 3 years in Korea, 2 more across a re-employment extension of roughly 1 year and 10 months. Add those two periods together and E-9 status tops out at around 4 years and 10 months before you'd need a different visa route to stay longer.

The one thing that doesn't count against either cap: changes caused by your employer's fault — the second category above. If your employer closed the business or violated your working conditions, that change is free; it doesn't use up one of your 3 or 2 chances.

## When it goes wrong

**You're past the 1 month application deadline because you didn't know the clock had started.** The statute doesn't give a general grace period outside the injury/illness/pregnancy/childbirth exception — this is one case where showing up at your local Employment Center immediately, rather than waiting to see if anyone notices, is the only lever you have. Bring your terminated contract and ask the caseworker directly whether your specific delay fits any administrative exception; don't assume it doesn't just because the law text doesn't name your situation.

**Your employer won't confirm the contract actually ended, so your 1 month clock never starts cleanly.** Get something in writing — a termination notice, a text message, anything dated — and bring it to the Employment Center yourself. The clock runs from when the contract ended as a matter of fact, not from when your employer agrees to paperwork.

**Immigration hasn't ruled on your transfer permit and you're approaching the 3 month mark.** That clock is about receiving the permit, not about how long processing is allowed to take — a slow case doesn't reset it on its own. Follow up with your Employment Center or the HiKOREA center handling your file before the 3 months run out, and keep your application receipt as proof of your filing date.

**Your employer is blocking the change itself** — refusing to release you, pressuring you to withdraw the application, or sitting on required paperwork. Obstructing a worker's Article 25 workplace change is a criminal offense under [제29조](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/외국인근로자의고용등에관한법률/제29조): up to **1 year in prison or a fine of up to 10,000,000 won**. Report it to the Employment Center handling your case or call the labor counseling line at **1350**; this isn't something you have to negotiate directly with the employer.

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Confirmed which of the three legal grounds your situation falls under</li>
  <li>Noted the exact date your contract ended — that's when the 1 month clock starts</li>
  <li>Filed the 사업장 변경신청서 with your local Employment Center within 1 month</li>
  <li>Tracked the 3 month clock for receiving your Article 21 transfer permit from the filing date</li>
  <li>Checked whether this change counts against your 3-time (initial period) or 2-time (re-employment) limit</li>
  <li>Saved your contract, termination notice, and application receipt in case of a dispute</li>
</ul>
