Permanent residency in Korea doesn't come with a renewal date on the status itself — the F-5 category never expires the way a work visa does. That single fact gets stretched into a myth: that F-5 is a finish line nothing can touch once you cross it. It isn't. Korea's Immigration Control Act lists nineteen separate paths into F-5, three baseline conditions every path has to clear, and five specific grounds the Ministry of Justice can use to cancel it again after approval. This guide covers all three — who actually qualifies, what it costs, and what happens if the application or the status itself goes wrong.

## Three baseline requirements before any route matters

[Article 10-3 of the Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1000817607&chrClsCd=010202) sets three conditions that apply regardless of which of the nineteen routes below you qualify under:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>품행 단정 — conduct the Ministry of Justice considers law-abiding, assessed from your criminal record and immigration history</li>
  <li>생계유지능력 — the ability to support yourself (or be supported by family you live with) through income or assets, without relying on public assistance</li>
  <li>기본소양 — basic familiarity with the Korean language and Korean society, most commonly proven by completing Korea's Social Integration Program (KIIP)</li>
</ul>

The law carves out one exemption from the second and third conditions: a foreign investor who has put in 500,000 US dollars or more under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act and employs 5 or more Korean nationals can have the income and basic-competency requirements relaxed. Everyone else clears all three.

## Nineteen routes, but most readers fit one of four

The actual eligibility table — [출입국관리법 시행령 별표 1의3](https://law.go.kr/flDownload.do?gubun=&flSeq=85170247), last amended December 12, 2023 — runs to nineteen numbered categories, covering everything from overseas doctorates to research staff at designated R&D facilities. Four routes account for most applicants:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li><strong>General long-term route:</strong> an adult who has held D-7 (intra-company transferee) through E-7 (specific activities) status, or F-2 (long-term residency points-based) status, for 5 years or more</li>
  <li><strong>Family route:</strong> the spouse or minor child of a Korean national or an F-5 holder, with 2 years or more of residence in Korea — this is the path most F-6 marriage-visa holders eventually take</li>
  <li><strong>Overseas-Korean route:</strong> an F-4 holder with 2 years or more of continuous residence, where the Ministry of Justice recognizes an ongoing need to stay</li>
  <li><strong>Investor route:</strong> an investor under the Foreign Investment Promotion Act with 500,000 US dollars or more invested and 5 or more Korean employees</li>
</ul>

A fifth, narrower route covers corporate investors on D-8 (기업투자) status under item 가목: 3 years or more of continuous stay, plus attracting 300,000,000 won or more in investment and employing 2 or more Korean nationals. Several more of the nineteen items require the investor or degree-holder to also keep the underlying condition — a minimum investment, a research post — in place for 5 years or longer, which matters because breaking that condition is itself one of the grounds for cancellation covered below.

## The income test doesn't have one published number worth repeating here

The 생계유지능력 requirement is usually measured against a multiple of the prior year's national per-capita gross national income, and that reference figure is recalculated every year. Several visa-consulting sites quote a specific won amount for the general route, but none of them point to a Ministry of Justice page I could independently confirm still carries that figure, and one source I checked produced an internally inconsistent calculation of its own cited base number. Rather than print a figure that might already be stale or wrong, the accurate answer is: check HiKorea's own 체류자격별 안내매뉴얼 (status-by-status manual) at [hikorea.go.kr](https://www.hikorea.go.kr), or call **1345** (외국인종합안내센터), for the number that applies the year you actually file.

## What KIIP buys you toward the "basic competency" requirement

Korea's Social Integration Program (사회통합프로그램, KIIP) runs on a legal basis of [Article 39 and Article 40 of the Immigration Control Act](https://www.immigration.go.kr/bbs/moj/184/474732/download.do), and completing it satisfies the 기본소양 requirement automatically — no separate test needed on top of it. The program runs through six stages:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>0단계 (basic): 15 hours, for those who score under 3 on the placement pre-evaluation</li>
  <li>1단계 through 4단계 (elementary through intermediate): 100 hours each, roughly matched to TOPIK levels 1 through 3</li>
  <li>5단계 (advanced): a 70-hour 기본과정 (basic track) plus a 30-hour 심화과정 (enrichment track) — a high enough pre-evaluation score lets you skip straight into this stage without the lower ones</li>
</ul>

After 5단계's basic track, you sit a government-run **영주용 종합평가** (comprehensive evaluation for permanent residency), separate from the 귀화용 종합평가 used for naturalization. Registration runs through socinet.go.kr, and the evaluation itself through kiiptest.org, both using the same ID; either portal's helpdesk and **1345** can confirm your specific pass threshold, since program operators update it periodically and I'd rather send you to the current number than print one here.

## What it costs, and which case is cheaper than you'd expect

If you're already in Korea on a registered status and applying to change into F-5, [시행규칙 제72조 of the Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?chrClsCd=010202&lsJoLnkSeq=1000817401) sets the fee at **200,000 won** — double the standard 100,000 won status-change fee our [guide to changing visa status](/change-visa-status-korea-foreigners/) covers for every other category. That surcharge is specific to F-5; nothing else on the fee schedule doubles like this.

There's one case that costs less, not more: a [separate gov.kr civil-service listing](https://www.gov.kr/portal/service/serviceInfo/PTR000050537) covers 체류자격 부여 (status *grant*, as opposed to *change*) for short-term stayers moving into F-2 or F-5 — most commonly a child born in Korea to a parent who already holds F-5. That filing runs **80,000 won**, with processing time listed the same way as every other immigration filing on gov.kr: "미정" (undetermined), typically **3 weeks to 3 months**. The same vague-but-bounded window applies to the ordinary F-5 status-change application — there's no fixed clock the way a stay extension has, because the review depends on which of the nineteen routes you're being evaluated under.

## Two privileges F-5 actually carries day to day

F-5 status itself has no stay-period sticker to renew, but the physical card does: our [ARC reissue guide](/arc-card-reissue-renewal-korea/) covers the **10-year** reissue cycle Article 33 sets specifically for permanent-resident registration cards — a rule that doesn't apply to any other status. And if you travel, our [re-entry permit guide](/re-entry-permit-korea-exemption-foreigners/) confirms F-5 holders get a **2-year** permit-free return window, double the 1-year window most other registered statuses get. Work rights are unrestricted too: F-5, along with F-2 and F-6, is exempt from the Article 20 side-income filing our [freelance income tax guide](/freelance-income-tax-korea-foreigners/) covers for every other visa category.

## When it goes wrong

**Your application is denied.** The office issues a written denial stating the reason — usually that the route you applied under isn't actually satisfied yet, most often the residency-length or income test. Ask directly which of the three baseline requirements or nineteen table items the denial cites; a shortfall on one specific item is a different problem than a blanket rejection, and the fix (wait out the remaining time, gather different income documentation, finish KIIP) depends entirely on which one it was.

**You can't confirm the current income or asset figure before applying.** Don't guess at a number from a blog and don't submit a round figure hoping it clears review. Confirm the year-specific amount through HiKorea's manual or **1345** first — applying against a wrong number wastes the 3-week-to-3-month review cycle rather than saving it.

**Your status gets cancelled after approval.** [Article 89-2 of the Immigration Control Act](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1000823214&chrClsCd=010202) limits cancellation to five grounds: obtaining F-5 through fraud or false documents (the one mandatory cancellation on the list); a conviction carrying 2 years or more in prison for specified serious crimes; a combined prison term of 3 years or more across convictions within the past 5 years; breaking a condition — most often an investment-maintenance requirement — your F-5 was granted on; or conduct the Ministry of Justice finds contrary to national security, diplomatic relations, or the national economy. If your F-5 is cancelled and you still need to stay, the same article lets you apply for an ordinary long-term status instead, rather than starting over from a short-term visa.

**You're an investor-route holder and your investment or employee count slips below the threshold.** That's condition-violation territory under ground four above, and it applies for as long as the specific route you used requires the condition to hold — in most investor categories, 5 years. Fix the shortfall and document it before an audit catches it rather than after.

**You're planning an extended trip and you're not sure the 2-year re-entry window covers it.** Apply for a re-entry permit before you leave if your trip might run past 2 years, since missing that window risks your registration being cancelled on a completely separate legal basis from the five grounds above.
