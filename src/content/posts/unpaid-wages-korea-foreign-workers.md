Our [severance pay guide](/korea-severance-pay-foreign-workers/) covers what you're owed when you leave a job. This one covers what to do when your employer simply doesn't pay — a missed payday, a shorted paycheck, unpaid overtime, or severance that never arrives. That situation has a name under Korean law, 임금체불 (wage arrears), and as of October 8, 2026 the penalty for it is steeper than it was a month ago. Nationality and visa status don't change any of this: the Labor Standards Act applies to every worker in Korea, documented or not.

## What the law actually requires

[근로기준법 제43조](https://casenote.kr/법령/근로기준법/제43조) sets four rules for every payday: wages must be paid in currency (통화불), directly to the worker (직접불), in full (전액불), and at least once a month on a fixed date (정기불). Deviating from any of these — paying in goods, routing pay through a third party, docking an unexplained amount, or skipping a scheduled payday — is a violation on its own, separate from whether you eventually get paid at all.

When employment ends, a separate rule kicks in: [근로기준법 제36조](https://casenote.kr/법령/근로기준법/제36조) requires your employer to settle all wages, unused leave payouts and other compensation within **14 days** of your last working day, extendable only by mutual agreement — not unilaterally because payroll is behind. This 14-day clock is the same one our severance guide covers for 퇴직금 specifically; Article 36 is the broader rule that covers your final regular paycheck too, not just severance.

You don't have unlimited time to chase an unpaid amount, either. [근로기준법 제49조](https://casenote.kr/법령/근로기준법/제49조) states plainly: "이 법에 따른 임금채권은 3년간 행사하지 아니하면 시효로 소멸한다" — a wage claim extinguishes if you don't act on it within **3 years**.

## What changed on October 8, 2026

A 2026.4.7 amendment to the Labor Standards Act (Law No. 21533) restructured the penalty for wage non-payment, effective October 8, 2026. The old [제109조](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkProc.do?lsNm=%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95&joNo=010900&mode=11) no longer covers Article 36 violations at all — that penalty moved to a new [제107조](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsLinkProc.do?lsNm=%EA%B7%BC%EB%A1%9C%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80%EB%B2%95&joNo=010700&mode=11), and the ceiling went up at the same time: violating Article 36 (final-wage settlement), Article 43 (the four payment rules above) or several related provisions now carries **up to 5 years in prison or a 50,000,000 won fine**, roughly 67% higher than the previous 3-year/30,000,000-won ceiling. The law still includes a 반의사불벌 clause — prosecution stops if the worker explicitly doesn't want it to proceed — except for employers already publicly named as habitual wage delinquents under Article 43-2, where prosecution goes ahead regardless of the worker's wishes.

## How to actually file a complaint

You have two routes, confirmed on [easylaw.go.kr's plain-language guide to wage-arrears remedies](https://www.easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMain.laf?csmSeq=1694&ccfNo=3&cciNo=4&cnpClsNo=1), run by the Ministry of Government Legislation:

- **Online**: file a 진정 (complaint) through the Ministry of Employment and Labor's own site (moel.go.kr) → e-민원마당 → wage-arrears complaint form.
- **In person**: visit the customer support desk at the regional employment and labor office covering your workplace's address, for a consultation before you file.

Either way, a 근로감독관 (labor inspector) is assigned to investigate. They can summon both sides and any witnesses; if you — the complainant — fail to appear after being summoned twice, the case can be closed without a finding. A 진정 is the administrative route: it asks the government to get your wages paid. A separate option, 고소 (criminal complaint), asks for your employer to be prosecuted under Article 107 instead; if you file that route, the investigation has to wrap up and refer the case to prosecutors within **2 months**. The same source confirms a **25-day** processing deadline for 진정 cases, counted from the date of filing, extendable once within that window if the inspector needs more time.

Once the inspector confirms the amount you're owed, the office issues a **체불 임금등 사업주 확인서** — an official certificate stating your employer failed to pay you and how much. That document is what unlocks the next steps below.

## If your employer still won't pay

A confirmed complaint doesn't put money in your account by itself — it opens up the collection options:

- **간이대지급금** (simplified wage replacement payment), run through the Korea Workers' Compensation and Welfare Service (근로복지공단) — also the agency behind [industrial accident insurance](/workplace-injury-insurance-korea-foreign-workers/) — pays unpaid wages and severance on an insolvent or uncooperative employer's behalf. The cap is **7,000,000 won** for wages and 휴업수당 covering your last 3 months, plus **7,000,000 won** for severance covering your last 3 years, for a combined maximum of **10,000,000 won**. You apply after your labor-office complaint produces the 체불 임금등 사업주 확인서, either through [근로복지공단's portal](https://total.comwel.or.kr) or at a regional branch, and you need to file within **1 year of a final civil judgment** or **6 months of the certificate's first issuance**, whichever applies to your case.
- **Civil suit.** If the amount owed is **30,000,000 won or less**, [소액사건심판규칙 제1조의2](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW/lsSideInfoP.do?lsiSeq=187995&joNo=0001&joBrNo=02&docCls=jo&urlMode=lsScJoRltInfoR) puts your case in the small-claims track — a simpler, faster civil procedure than ordinary litigation. Above that threshold, the case goes through regular civil court instead, and you can't split one larger claim into smaller pieces just to stay under the limit.
- **Interest on a late severance payment.** If the unpaid amount is specifically severance pay and your employer missed the 14-day deadline without your agreement to extend it, our severance guide (linked below) covers the 20% annual interest that accrues on the unpaid balance from day 15 onward.

## If your visa status is the reason you haven't reported it

A common fear keeps people from filing at all: that reporting unpaid wages will expose an expired visa or unregistered status and trigger deportation. As of a rule change effective **November 6, 2025**, that calculus changed. [Korea's official policy briefing](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148954167) confirms the Ministry of Justice amended the Enforcement Rule of the Immigration Act so that labor inspectors handling a wage-arrears complaint are now exempt from the general duty public officials have to report any undocumented status they discover in the course of their work. The background given is explicit: workers were abandoning legitimate wage claims because reporting them risked forced departure. Foreign workers already under a deportation order who come forward with a wage complaint can also have their detention temporarily released at the discretion of the local immigration office, per the same briefing. This exemption covers the labor-office complaint process specifically — it doesn't rewrite your underlying visa situation, so pair a wage complaint with advice on your status if you're not sure where you stand; our [visa status change guide](/change-visa-status-korea-foreigners/) covers the Article 94(12) penalty for separate unauthorized-activity issues.

## When it goes wrong

**The inspector summoned you and you missed it twice.** The case can be closed without a finding (내사종결) if you don't respond to two summons for your own statement. If you missed both by accident — wrong contact number on file, a shift schedule conflict — contact the office directly and ask to reopen or refile rather than assuming the case is permanently dead.

**Your 진정 was resolved but the amount looks wrong.** Ask the inspector for the calculation behind the 체불 임금등 사업주 확인서 in writing before accepting it as final; a wrong 3-month average-wage figure is a common, fixable error, not evidence of a dead end.

**Your 간이대지급금 application got denied for a document issue.** Ask the caseworker exactly which document was missing or wrong — a denial is usually fixable by resubmitting with the right paperwork, provided you're still inside the 1-year/6-month filing window above.

**You're close to the 3-year limit and nothing has happened yet.** File the 진정 now rather than waiting for a "better" time to deal with it — Article 49's 3-year clock runs from when each wage payment became due, not from when you get around to complaining, and there's no late exception once it lapses.

  <li>Confirm your pay violates one of the four payment rules (통화불·직접불·전액불·정기불) or missed the 14-day post-termination deadline</li>
  <li>File a 진정 online via moel.go.kr or in person at the labor office covering your workplace's address</li>
  <li>Keep payslips, your contract and any message record of promised-but-missed payment — the inspector will ask for these</li>
  <li>Once you have a 체불 임금등 사업주 확인서, apply for 간이대지급금 within 6 months, or pursue civil recovery within the 30,000,000 won small-claims threshold if higher</li>
  <li>If an unresolved visa issue is the reason you've held off reporting, note that the labor office's notification exemption (effective Nov 6, 2025) applies specifically to wage-arrears complaints</li>
  <li>Track the 3-year statute of limitations from each missed payday, not from today</li>
</ul>
