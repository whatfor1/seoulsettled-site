Handing a landlord a deposit worth hundreds of millions of won, then trusting they'll give it back years later, is the single strangest part of renting in Korea to anyone who didn't grow up with jeonse. Most of the time it works out. When it doesn't, getting a Korean court to force repayment can take months — which is exactly the gap that guarantee insurance is built to close.

## What this insurance actually does

전세보증금반환보증 (jeonse deposit return guarantee insurance) is a product you buy that promises: if your landlord doesn't return your deposit when the lease ends, the guarantor pays you directly, then goes after the landlord themselves. You're not waiting on your landlord's finances or a lawsuit — you're collecting from an institution that's contractually on the hook. Two organizations sell it in Korea: HUG (주택도시보증공사, a public housing-finance corporation) and SGI Seoul Guarantee (SGI서울보증보험, a private insurer). A third option, HF's 전세지킴보증, is bundled into some bank jeonse loans, but HUG and SGI are the ones you buy on your own.

## HUG: the public option, and its limits

HUG's 전세보증금반환보증 is the one most tenants reach for first, and the [Seoul city government's tenant-protection page](https://housing.seoul.go.kr/site/main/content/sh01_060400) lays out the eligibility plainly: your deposit has to be **700 million won (700,000,000 won) or less in the Seoul metro area (Seoul, Gyeonggi, Incheon)**, or **500 million won (500,000,000 won) or less everywhere else**, and the deposit plus any loans already secured against the property can't exceed **90% of the home's assessed value**. The same page gives a worked example: on a home valued at 100 million won, the guarantee limit is 90 million won — a deposit of 95 million won would be turned down, 90 million won would pass. HUG covers apartments, officetels used as housing, and multi-household (다세대/연립) or single/multi-family (단독/다가구) housing that's registered as such.

The fee itself scales with how risky your particular unit looks. [HUG's own published rate table](https://www.khug.or.kr/hug/web/ig/dr/igdr000001.jsp), for a 1-year guarantee, breaks it down by how much of the home's value your deposit represents:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Deposit at 70% or less of home value: 0.097% a year for apartments, 0.111% for other housing</li>
  <li>70–80%: 0.117% for apartments, 0.142% for other housing</li>
  <li>Over 80%: 0.137% for apartments, 0.172% for other housing</li>
</ul>

On a 300,000,000 won deposit in an apartment at the lowest risk tier, that's roughly 291,000 won a year — not nothing, but a lot cheaper than losing the deposit outright. You apply through HUG's own channels, the Kakao-linked 모바일 전세보증금반환보증 app, or a partnered bank branch, before half of your lease term has elapsed from whichever date comes later: your move-in payment date or your 전입신고 (address registration) date. On a standard 2-year lease, that's roughly the 1-year mark — don't wait past it.

## SGI: faster to qualify, pricier to hold

SGI Seoul Guarantee doesn't apply the same deposit ceiling for apartments — it will guarantee the full deposit amount regardless of size, while other housing types are capped at **1 billion won (1,000,000,000 won)**. The trade-off is price: a March 2025 rate revision reported by [Nate News's coverage of the SGI premium hike](https://news.nate.com/view/20250326n27243) raised SGI's apartment rate from 0.183% to **0.229%** a year, and its non-apartment rate from 0.208% to **0.260%**, before LTV-based discounts (up to 30% off if your deposit sits at 50% or less of the home's value) or surcharges for riskier units. SGI also wants your lease signed through a licensed real estate agent and your 전입신고 and 확정일자 (notarized date stamp on the lease) already done before you apply, and — like HUG — you need to apply within half the lease term, with a separate rule allowing application within the first 12 months of any lease longer than 24 months.

## The government will refund part of the fee

If you're a tenant who doesn't own property, Korea's housing ministry runs a subsidy, listed on [gov.kr's guarantee-fee support page](https://www.gov.kr/portal/rcvfvrSvc/dtlEx/161300000103), that reimburses the premium you already paid through your local 시·군·구청 or 주민센터: up to **400,000 won** (raised from 300,000 won for policies bought before March 30, 2025), covering the full fee for renters under a youth-income line (연소득 5,000만원 이하) or newlyweds (7,500만원 이하), and 90% of the fee — again capped at 400,000 won — for everyone else earning under 6,000만원 a year, on deposits up to 300,000,000 won. It's processed as a reimbursement after you've already paid for the guarantee, decided within **30 days** of your application (extendable once by 15 more days), and it's first-come-first-served against each local government's annual budget — so apply within weeks of paying the premium, not months later.

## Applying before you need it

Both insurers want the same basic paperwork: your lease contract, your ARC (as the official ID for a foreign tenant), proof you've completed 전입신고, and — for HUG especially — the building's registry (등기부등본) and building ledger (건축물대장) showing it's legally registered housing. If you're renting for the first time, work through [how to rent in Korea with jeonse or wolse](/jeonse-wolse-renting-apartment-korea-foreigners/) before this step, since the lease terms you negotiate determine whether you even qualify. And keep the guarantee fee payment coming out of the same account you used to [open your Korean bank account](/open-bank-account-korea-foreigner/), since both insurers ask for proof of payment tied to a named account.

## When it goes wrong: the landlord doesn't pay

**If your lease ends and the deposit doesn't show up.** Under HUG's own guarantee terms, a claim can be filed starting exactly **1 month** after the lease ends without repayment, and HUG is contractually meant to pay out within **1 month** of that claim being accepted. In practice, that promise is often missed: a National Assembly audit cited by [Kukmin Ilbo's reporting on HUG payout delays](https://www.kmib.co.kr/article/view.asp?arcid=1744104668&code=11131100&sid1=soc&stg=ws_real) found that of 1,821 claims reviewed, **1,037 cases — past the 1-month deadline, a 57%** miss rate — and **153 cases** ran longer than **6 months**, with HUG staff telling some claimants to expect **4 months to 5 months**. If your payout is late, that 1-month contractual window is the number to cite when you call HUG and ask what's holding your specific claim up — budget for the delay rather than counting on the money landing on a fixed date.

**If you need to move out before the deposit is repaid.** Do not submit a 전출 (change of address) or move your 전입신고 to a new address until you've either been repaid in full or completed a [임차권등기명령 (lease registration order)](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMain.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=629&ccfNo=5&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1) on the old address. Moving out and re-registering your address elsewhere before doing one of those two things can cost you your 대항력 (right to assert your lease against a new owner) and 우선변제권 (priority to be repaid from an auction) on the old unit — the two legal protections your entire deposit claim depends on. This is the one jeonse rule that cuts directly against the [15-day address-change requirement](/report-address-change-korea-arc-15-days/) you'd otherwise rush to meet after any other move.

**If you need to file for 임차권등기명령.** You apply at the district court, branch court, or municipal court covering the property's location, and only after the lease has already ended without repayment. Once the registration is complete, the law preserves your 대항력 and 우선변제권 even if you later lose the occupancy requirement by moving — and it lets you bill the landlord for the registration costs afterward.

**If your landlord turns out to be running a multi-property scheme and your claim is denied or stuck.** Korea's 전세사기 피해자 지원 및 주거안정에 관한 특별법 (jeonse fraud victim support act) has been extended through **May 31, 2027**. An enforcement decree amendment, approved by the Cabinet on October 6, 2026 and reported by [GNN News](https://www.gnnnews.kr/206716), takes effect **November 13, 2026** — not yet in force as of this writing — and adds a "최소보장제" (minimum-guarantee) rule: if what you recover after your home is sold at auction still falls short of **one-third** of your deposit, the government covers the gap. A separate "선지급-후정산" (pay-first, settle-later) track under the same amendment lets victims of a specific fraud pattern — landlords who leased out units they had no legal right to rent — get that same minimum-guarantee amount paid out by LH (Korea Land and Housing Corporation) before the auction even finishes, with LH deciding within **90 days** of the application. If you suspect your landlord owns an unusually large number of units on cheap credit, or your insurance claim is rejected because the fraud pattern complicates the payout, that special-law support channel — not guarantee insurance alone — is what to ask your local 전세사기피해지원센터 about.
