The bank account is open, the debit card works, and then the credit card application filled in at the same counter comes back rejected — no real explanation, just a form letter or a shrug from the teller. That's not a one-off. Korea's Financial Supervisory Service guidebook for banks states plainly that credit card applicants "must meet conditions on sojourn status, job, property, financial transaction records," on top of the identity check every card type needs. A debit card skips all of that because it only ever spends money already in the account. A credit card is Korea's financial system deciding whether to lend to you, and a resident of 6 weeks with no Korean paycheck history looks, on paper, exactly like a credit risk.

## Why the rejection happens

Korea's two private credit bureaus, KCB and NICE, build your score from domestic financial activity tied to your resident or foreign-registration number: phone contracts, utility accounts, past loans, card repayment history. Arrive with none of that and there's nothing for either bureau to score — not a bad score, just an absent one, which most banks treat the same way as a bad one. The [Seoul Metropolitan Government's banking guide](https://english.seoul.go.kr/service/living/convenience/1-banking) warns foreign residents directly that a thin Korean credit history "can get a credit-card request turned down," and recommends taking the debit card instead, since it needs no credit check at all.

## What you actually need before you apply

There's no single published checklist, but the same documents come up at every bank: your Alien Registration Card, a certificate of employment or income proof (재직증명서, a tax withholding receipt, or 3 months of pay slips), and visa time remaining on your card — card companies are reluctant to approve someone whose stay could legally end before the bill does. This overlaps almost exactly with the proof-of-purpose paperwork the [bank account guide](/open-bank-account-korea-foreigner/) already covers, so bring the same folder.

Card type matters too. The government's own [Living in Korea guide](https://www.investkorea.org/upload/kotraexpress/2022/05/images/Living_in_Korea.pdf), sourced from the FSS Financial Guidebook, lays out the difference:

| | Credit card | Check / debit card | Prepaid card |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spending limit | Credit limit | Account balance | Loaded balance |
| Billed | On a set monthly day | Immediately | Immediately |
| Cash advance | Possible | Not possible | Not possible |
| Who can get one | Must clear sojourn status, job, property and transaction-record checks | Opens with a bank account | Opens with a bank account |

That bottom row is the whole story: a check card rides on the account you already have, a credit card is underwritten separately.

## Check your score before you waste a trip

Korea's Credit Information Use and Protection Act gives everyone, residents included, 3 free credit report checks a year — once every 4 months — through [allcredit.co.kr](https://www.allcredit.co.kr/screen/sc9418185040), KCB's own consumer portal. Verification runs through mobile-phone authentication, so this only works once you have a Korean number in your own name — which itself needs either an ARC or, per the [phone number guide](/korean-phone-number-foreigners-prepaid-postpaid/), a postpaid plan that already needs one. Apps like Toss and KakaoBank also surface your KCB score for free once you're signed up. If the score comes back blank rather than low, a card rejection isn't really about you — it's that the file is empty, and the fix is building a paper trail (a phone plan, a utility account, a few months of salary landing in the same bank account), not reapplying with the same blank file next week.

## The workaround: foreigner-only check cards

If credit approval doesn't come through, several banks issue check cards built specifically for foreign residents rather than the standard account debit card. KB Kookmin Bank's foreigner-only "KB국민 웰컴 플러스" check card is one example: issued to foreigners aged 18 years and up, no issuance fee and no annual fee, applied for at a KB branch, with English and Chinese phone support through KB's Global Customer Center at 1644-9993. Its discount terms (weekend purchases of 30,000 won or more at Emart, Lotte Mart or Homeplus get 5% off, capped at 5,000 won per transaction; pharmacy purchases of 30,000 won or more get the same 5% up to 2,500 won) are tied to spending at least 200,000 won the previous month, rising to a 20,000-won monthly discount cap above 300,000 won — card terms shift over the years, so confirm the current numbers with the branch rather than assuming they're unchanged. The point isn't this specific card; it's that "no credit card" doesn't mean "no card that works like one for daily spending" — ask what foreigner-specific check card a bank offers before settling for the plain debit card.

## The application in order

1. **Build a paper trail first if you're under 6 months in Korea.** A Korean phone contract and at least one full salary deposit cycle through your bank account both feed KCB and NICE.
2. **Check your score for free** at allcredit.co.kr or in Toss/KakaoBank before applying anywhere, so you know whether you're walking in with an empty file or a weak one.
3. **Bring the full folder to the branch:** ARC, passport, certificate of employment or pay slips, and your bank account details. Card companies generally don't take foreigner applications by phone, only in person or through a linked bank's app.
4. **Apply at the bank you already use.** Shinhan, KB, Woori and Hana all issue cards to their own account holders, and an existing transaction history with that bank counts toward the approval.
5. **Expect roughly a week to ten days for a decision.** None of the official sources publish a guaranteed turnaround, so ask the branch for this application's specific timeline rather than assuming.
6. **If declined, ask for the foreigner-specific check card** instead of walking out with nothing.

## If the application goes wrong

**You're rejected and no one explains why.** Ask the branch to confirm whether it's a thin-file issue (nothing to score) or a declined score (something negative on file). The first is fixed by time and a transaction history; the second needs you to find out what's on the report via allcredit.co.kr and correct or wait it out.

**Your ARC has less than 6 months left on it.** Several card companies are reluctant to extend credit past a visa's expiry, since they can't be sure of collecting afterward. Renew or extend your stay first — see the [visa extension guide](/extend-stay-period-korea-visa-extension-hikorea/) — then reapply.

**Your card is lost or stolen.** Call your card company's hotline immediately, not your bank. The numbers differ by company: Shinhan Card's English line is 1544-7333, Samsung Card's is 1688-8751, KB Kookmin's English/Chinese line is 1644-9993, and Lotte Card's English line is 1899-8100. BC Card, Citi, NH Nonghyup, Woori and Hana cards each have their own separate lost-card line listed on the back of the card itself — call the number printed there, since a general bank hotline usually can't freeze a card issued by a different card company.

**You applied through an app and it won't let you finish.** Foreign-resident identity verification on banking and card apps checks your name against your phone carrier, your ID and your bank account, character for character including spacing. A mismatch anywhere in that chain fails the whole thing — fix the spelling at whichever one is wrong, then retry.

## Quick list before you apply

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Alien Registration Card with 6+ months of validity left</li>
  <li>Certificate of employment or pay slips covering the last 3 months</li>
  <li>Korean bank account already open, ideally with a few salary deposits in it</li>
  <li>Free credit score checked at allcredit.co.kr or in Toss/KakaoBank</li>
  <li>Korean phone number in your own name, spelling matching your ID exactly</li>
