Two completely different things get called "전세사기" (jeonse fraud) in casual conversation, and mixing them up wastes time. One is a check you run before you sign a lease, to avoid becoming a victim in the first place. The other is a legal status — 전세사기피해자 — that Korea's government decides you qualify for after the fact, which unlocks a specific set of support programs under a dedicated special law. This guide covers both: the free prevention tool to run before you commit a deposit, and what the victim-support system actually pays out if prevention is already too late.

## Before you sign: run 안심전세

안심전세 (Anshim Jeonse, "safe jeonse") is a free app built by HUG (주택도시보증공사, Korea's public housing-finance corporation) specifically to catch the warning signs of jeonse fraud before money changes hands. Enter an address and it pulls together, in one screen, the checks a landlord is counting on you not making yourself:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Basic building information — what the unit is actually registered as, which matters because unregistered or misclassified units can fall outside guarantee-insurance eligibility entirely</li>
  <li>공시가격 (the government's officially assessed price) compared against market data, so you can see if the deposit you're being asked for is unusually close to or above what the home is actually worth</li>
  <li>선순위채권 and other 권리관계 — existing mortgages, liens, and legal claims already registered against the property that would be repaid before you in a forced sale</li>
  <li>The landlord's tax-delinquency status, a direct signal of the kind of multi-unit, cash-strapped landlord behind most jeonse fraud schemes</li>
</ul>

None of these checks replace a lawyer or a licensed real estate agent, and the app can only report what's already in official registries — it can't catch a landlord who hasn't yet fallen behind on anything. Read our [jeonse vs wolse guide](/jeonse-wolse-renting-apartment-korea-foreigners/) for how the deposit structure itself works before you get to this step, and our [guide to jeonse deposit guarantee insurance](/jeonse-deposit-guarantee-insurance-korea-hug-sgi/) for the separate product that pays you back even if a fraud check comes back clean and something still goes wrong.

## Do you qualify as a recognized victim?

If prevention didn't work, the next question is whether you meet the legal definition in [제3조 of the 전세사기피해자 지원 및 주거안정에 관한 특별법](https://www.law.go.kr/법령/전세사기피해자지원및주거안정에관한특별법/제3조) (Special Act on Support for Jeonse Fraud Victims and Housing Stability). Four conditions apply together, though two of them are waived if an auction or public sale on the unit has already finished:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>You hold a 주택임대차보호법 Article 3 protection — delivery of the unit plus 전입신고 (address registration), or a registered 임차권등기 or 전세권 — on the lease in question</li>
  <li>Your 임차보증금 (deposit) is <strong>500,000,000 won or less</strong>, though the regional 전세사기피해지원위원회 (victim support committee) can discretionarily raise that ceiling by up to <strong>200,000,000 won</strong> for a specific case, putting the practical cap as high as 700,000,000 won</li>
  <li>Your landlord is insolvent or bankrupt, an auction or public sale on the unit has already started (including a seizure for unpaid taxes), or two or more tenants in the same building suffered the same non-repayment harm</li>
  <li>There's reasonable grounds to suspect the landlord's fraudulent intent — an investigation has already started, there's evidence of deception, the unit was transferred to someone with no ability to repay, or the landlord acquired or leased out an unusually large number of units without the means to cover them</li>
</ul>

The same article excludes you if you already have guarantee insurance covering the full deposit, you qualify for 최우선변제 (top-priority repayment), or your 대항력/우선변제권 already lets you recover the full amount on your own — the program is built for the gap guarantee insurance doesn't cover, not as a second payout on top of it. Most jeonse leases in Korea run **2 years**, so it's worth checking where you stand against these four conditions well before your own lease term is up, rather than only after a dispute has already started.

## What recognized victims get

Once the committee determines you qualify, a few concrete benefits attach to that status, with sourcing quality that varies by item — I'm flagging the weaker ones rather than presenting every figure as equally solid:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li><strong>우선매수권:</strong> the right to buy your own unit at auction for the exact price the winning bidder offered, instead of having to outbid anyone. You can also transfer that right to LH (한국토지주택공사, Korea Land and Housing Corporation) within 7 days if you'd rather it buy the unit and lease it back to you</li>
  <li><strong>공공임대 전환:</strong> if LH ends up acquiring your former unit, confirmed terms from [korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr) give you up to <strong>10 years rent-free</strong> as public housing, with an optional second 10-year term at 50-70% of market rent</li>
  <li><strong>Auction/tax-sale deferral:</strong> up to <strong>1 year</strong>, extendable, per [easylaw.go.kr's summary of Articles 17-19](https://easylaw.go.kr) — this buys time rather than cancelling the sale outright</li>
  <li><strong>Local tax relief:</strong> an 취득세 (acquisition tax) exemption reported up to <strong>2,000,000 won</strong> and a multi-year 재산세 (property tax) reduction if you end up buying replacement housing — the exact reduction percentage varies by unit size and has been adjusted more than once, so confirm the current rate with your local 구청 tax division rather than relying on a fixed number here</li>
</ul>

## New from November 13, 2026: the minimum-guarantee top-up

A 2026 amendment to the special law adds a cash backstop that didn't exist before, confirmed directly by [법제처 (Ministry of Government Legislation), via korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148974760): if what you've actually recovered — through 대항력/우선변제권, an auction or public-sale dividend, or existing support programs — still falls short of **one-third of your original deposit**, the government pays the remaining gap in cash as a 최소지원금 (minimum support payment). A [government card-news release](https://www.korea.kr) gives the mechanism's name as 전세사기피해자 최소보장제 and confirms it takes effect **November 13, 2026**.

A second track, 선지급-후정산 (pay first, settle later), applies specifically if your fraud involved a landlord with no legal authority to lease the unit at all (a 신탁사기 pattern) — you can receive that same minimum-guarantee amount from LH before the auction or disposal process even finishes, with the government settling accounts afterward. Per the same government release, online pre-applications for both tracks opened through **LH 청약플러스** on **November 10, 2026**, with in-person applications at LH's regional offices starting on the law's November 13 effective date.

One figure I could not confirm on a primary government page, despite checking both the statute text and two korea.kr releases directly: multiple Korean news outlets reported that LH has **90 days** from your application to decide whether to pay out, extendable once by **30 days** if documentation needs supplementing. Treat that as a reporting-based planning estimate rather than a cited statute deadline, and confirm your specific case's timeline directly with LH or your regional support center when you apply.

## How and where to apply

Victim-determination applications go through the **전세사기피해자 지원관리시스템** at [jeonse.kgeop.go.kr](https://jeonse.kgeop.go.kr), which has accepted online applications since **April 25, 2024** as an alternative to visiting your regional government office in person — the portal's own call center is **1600-9640**. Two case-specific hotlines sit alongside it: **1533-8119** for help with victim determination and general support, and **1588-1663**, run by HUG, specifically for auction and public-sale support once a case is already moving through that process.

The application process has four distinct forms on the portal — a determination application (최초 신청), an objection if you disagree with the decision (이의신청), a correction request for details like a name change, and a withdrawal if you settle with the landlord before the process finishes. Required documents include the determination application form, a copy of your lease contract, and — if you don't consent to the agency pulling it electronically — a copy of your 등본 (resident registration extract).

Based on the portal's own published guidance rather than a direct statute citation, the review runs in two stages: your local government does an initial review within **30 days**, then a central-government committee makes the final determination within another **30 days**, extendable once by **15 days** if the case needs more documentation. Treat that as the shape of the process rather than a guaranteed clock, and ask your caseworker for a case-specific estimate once you've filed.

## When it goes wrong

**Your application is rejected.** The 이의신청 (objection) form on the same portal lets you request another review without starting over from scratch — use it before assuming the decision is final, and keep a copy of whatever rejection notice you receive, since it should state the specific reason.

**You're not sure you clear the deposit ceiling.** Don't assume a deposit over 500,000,000 won automatically disqualifies you — the +200,000,000 won discretionary adjustment exists for exactly this situation. Apply anyway and let the regional committee make that call rather than ruling yourself out first.

**Your landlord contests the fraud finding, or disputes the auction.** This is a separate process from your victim-status application — the committee's determination of victim status doesn't itself force a legal outcome against the landlord. Your 대항력 and 우선변제권 (if you have them) still run through the regular court/auction system, in parallel with the special-law support track.

**You already got a guarantee-insurance payout, and now wonder if you can also get the minimum-guarantee top-up.** Re-read the exclusions above: if guarantee insurance already covered your full deposit, you don't qualify for victim status in the first place, and the 1/3 minimum-guarantee rule only closes a gap that's still open — it isn't a second, stacked payout.

**You moved out and re-registered your address somewhere else before resolving the deposit.** This is the single most damaging mistake across every jeonse dispute, not just fraud cases — see the address-registration warning in our [guarantee insurance guide](/jeonse-deposit-guarantee-insurance-korea-hug-sgi/) before you do it, since it can cost you the legal protections your entire claim depends on.
