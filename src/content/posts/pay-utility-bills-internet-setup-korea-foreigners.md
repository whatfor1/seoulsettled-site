Four different companies bill you for one apartment: KEPCO for electricity, a regional gas company for heating and cooking gas, your district's waterworks office for water, and KT, SK Broadband or LG U+ for internet. None of them talk to each other, none of them send a combined bill, and each one has its own late-fee clock running the moment you move in. This is what each bill actually contains, what happens if you're late, and what the internet contract doesn't tell you until you try to cancel it.

## Electricity: KEPCO, and a late fee that compounds in two steps

Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO, 한국전력공사) is the only electricity provider in the country, so there's no choice to make here, only paperwork. New service is set up at [KEPCO's customer center](https://www.kepco.co.kr/home/customer/online/consultation/guide.do) online, through the 한전ON app, by phone at 123, or at a branch; you'll need ID (your ARC, [registered within 90 days of arrival](/how-to-get-arc-korea/), works fine) and a copy of your lease, and setup itself costs 0 won. Your bill is a basic charge plus a usage charge, and by law it also carries a 전력산업기반기금 (Electric Power Industry Fund) surcharge of 2.7% on top, per the Ministry of Justice's [easylaw.go.kr guide to household electricity rates](https://www.easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=1008&ccfNo=2&cciNo=1&cnpClsNo=1).

Miss the due date and KEPCO adds a 가산금 (late surcharge) automatically: 1.5% on top of the unpaid amount once you're a month overdue, rising to 2.5% total if you're still unpaid after two months, confirmed directly on [KEPCO's own billing page](https://home.kepco.co.kr/kepco/front/html/CY/E/D/CYEDHP00102.html). The same page lists the fix: enroll in automatic bank transfer and KEPCO takes 1% off your bill every month, capped at 1,000 won, which is a small discount but it also means you can't forget to pay. Credit card autopay doesn't qualify for that 1% discount — only direct bank transfer does.

## Gas: the company depends on your address, not your choice

Unlike electricity, gas is regional. In Seoul it's [Seoul City Gas](https://www.seoulgas.co.kr/) in most districts, with a different company (코원에너지서비스) covering a few areas like Gangnam and Seocho — check your building's gas meter sticker if you're not sure which one bills you. Seoul City Gas charges a late fee of 2% per month on the unpaid amount, calculated daily and capped at applying twice a year, stated directly on [the company's own payment-methods page](https://www.seoulgas.co.kr/front/customer/infoPay). That's a meaningfully higher rate than KEPCO's, so a gas bill is the one you want on autopay first.

Setting up autopay works through the same channels as checking your bill: the Seoul City Gas website, its mobile app, the "가스톡" KakaoTalk channel, a bank or card company counter, or the call center at 1588-5788. Applications submitted mid-month typically take effect the following billing cycle, not immediately, so set it up as soon as you move in rather than waiting for your first bill to arrive.

Autopay also quietly expires if you're not careful. A failed bank-transfer autopay reverts your account to manual 지로 billing after 6 months of missed payments in a row; a failed card autopay reverts after just 3 months, since cards expire more often than bank account numbers change.

Water is simpler in most apartments: it's billed through your building's monthly maintenance fee (관리비) rather than a separate account, so you may never deal with a water company directly unless you live in a single-family house, in which case your district's waterworks office (상수도사업본부) bills you the same way KEPCO does.

## Setting up home internet: the carrier matters more than the plan

KT, SK Broadband and LG U+ all sell home internet to foreign residents, but the paperwork differs by carrier. KT's own customer-service guide states that a foreign resident signing up in person needs only their ARC in their own name — [confirmed on KT's official sign-up document page](https://help.kt.com/serviceinfo/ServiceJoinGuideL4.do), which also covers signing up through a representative if you'd rather not do it yourself (ARC, passport, a signed consent matching your passport signature, and the representative's ID). SK Broadband and LG U+ are commonly reported to also require a Korean bank account opened using your ARC specifically — a bank account opened on a tourist visa with just a passport often isn't accepted for the autopay setup these carriers want. If you haven't [opened a bank account](/open-bank-account-korea-foreigner/) yet, that's the step to do first.

## The contract trap: why a "discount" is a bet on how long you'll stay

Every carrier prices home internet the same way: a higher sticker price with no contract, and increasingly large discounts for 1-, 2- or 3-year commitments. The 3-year discount looks the best on the monthly bill, and it's the one that costs the most if you break it. Walk away before the contract ends and you owe a 할인반환금 (discount clawback) — not a flat fine, but a repayment of some of the discount you already received.

That penalty structure changed in 2023, and the new version is friendlier than it used to be. The Korean government's own policy news service confirms the reform: on a contract that runs 36 months, penalties now shrink starting 18 months in and reach exactly 0 won by the end, instead of the older system where the penalty kept climbing until roughly 24 months had passed. [Korea.kr's coverage of the change](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148918180) lists the rollout dates by carrier: KT from September 8, 2023, SK Broadband and SKT from September 27, 2023, and LG U+ from November 1, 2023 — all carriers you'd sign up with today are on this newer, lower-penalty schedule. Cancel 4 months into a 36-month plan and you'll owe most of the discount back; cancel at 30 months and you'll owe comparatively little.

One exception that waives the penalty outright: if you're leaving Korea for good. Carriers accept proof of emigration or a confirmed departure (your ARC plus documentation showing you're relocating abroad) as grounds to cancel without the discount clawback — ask the carrier's call center what exactly they'll accept before your last month, since the required paperwork is handled case by case rather than listed on a single public page.

> **Jun's tip:** If you're not sure how long you'll be in Korea, a contract term of 12 months costs more per month than one running 36 months, but it caps your worst-case penalty at a fraction of what a broken 36-month deal would cost. The difference in monthly price is usually smaller than the difference in exit risk.

## If a bill arrives you don't recognize, or the name on the account is wrong

The most common utility problem for a new arrival isn't a high bill, it's a bill addressed to someone else — the previous tenant, or a name that doesn't match your ARC. Fix this before you pay anything: changing the name on a KEPCO account requires your ID, a copy of your lease, and the customer number from the bill itself, and can be done at a branch or through 한전ON. For gas and internet, call the provider directly; all three expect the account holder's name to match the person actually living there, and an account still under a previous tenant's name can cause problems if you ever need to prove your address for immigration paperwork.

If an autopay attempt fails — insufficient funds, an expired card — don't assume it will just retry silently forever; as the gas section above shows, a few missed months quietly turns "automatic" back into "manual." Check your payment method is still valid whenever you get a new bank card.

If you're disputing a bill you think is wrong — a reading that looks too high, or charges from before you moved in — ask the provider for an 이사정산 (move settlement) based on your actual move-in date and meter reading, not the billing cycle's default dates. This is standard enough that KEPCO's move-in/move-out settlement process costs 0 won and is handled through the same 123 customer center or 한전ON app as everything else.

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Set up electricity (KEPCO) through 한전ON, the app, or 123 — ARC and lease copy, no fee</li>
  <li>Confirm which gas company covers your address from the meter sticker, not assumption</li>
  <li>Put your gas bill on autopay first — its 2% monthly late fee is higher than electricity's 1.5%</li>
  <li>Open a bank account with your ARC before signing up for SK Broadband or LG U+ internet</li>
  <li>Pick a 1-year internet contract if you're not sure how long you'll stay in Korea</li>
  <li>Change the account name immediately if a bill arrives under a previous tenant's name</li>
  <li>Ask for a free, 0 won 이사정산 (move settlement) if a reading looks wrong</li>
