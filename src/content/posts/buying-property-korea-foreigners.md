Nothing in Korean law stops a foreigner from buying an apartment, a house, or land — ownership itself isn't restricted by nationality, and our [renting guide](/jeonse-wolse-renting-apartment-korea-foreigners/) covers the completely separate jeonse/wolse system for people who aren't buying. What trips people up with buying is the paperwork layered on top: a report due within 60 days of signing, a permit you need **before** you sign in some areas, and — since August 2025 — a flat requirement to actually live in anything you buy across Seoul, Incheon, and most of Gyeonggi for two years straight.

## Three separate rulebooks, not one

Every foreign buyer runs into the first rule below. Most never hit the second. Anyone buying housing in the Seoul metro area now has to deal with the third, and it's the one with real teeth:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>A nationwide acquisition report, due within 60 days of your contract — applies everywhere, every type of property</li>
  <li>A land acquisition permit, required before signing, but only inside specific restricted zones (military, cultural heritage, ecological, wildlife areas)</li>
  <li>A land transaction permit for housing specifically in Seoul, Incheon, and most of Gyeonggi, in force since August 26, 2025 and renewed through August 25, 2027</li>
</ul>

## Report every purchase within 60 days

Under [Article 8 of the Real Estate Transaction Reporting Act, summarized on easylaw.go.kr](https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMain.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=1259&ccfNo=1&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=1): "외국인등이 대한민국 안의 부동산등을 취득하는 계약을 체결하였을 때에는 계약체결일부터 60일 이내에 신고관청에 신고해야 합니다" — if you're a foreigner (or foreign-majority company) who signs a contract to acquire real estate anywhere in Korea, you must report it to the relevant city/county/district office within **60 days** of the contract date. This applies to an ordinary purchase contract and to a gift contract alike.

Skip it, report late, or report falsely, and [the Incheon Bupyeong district office's civil-service page](https://www.icbp.go.kr/main/civil/land/foreigner.jsp) confirms the fine: up to **3,000,000 won**. Processing on a correctly filed report is fast — the same page lists it as immediate, typically handled within about 3 hours at the counter.

## Inherited, auctioned, or already Korean-owned: the clock is 6 months

Not every acquisition comes from a signed purchase contract. If you end up owning Korean real estate through inheritance, a court auction, exercising a right of redemption, a final court judgment, or a corporate merger, the same Article 8 gives you **6 months** from the date you actually acquired it to file, not 60 days. The fine for missing that window is lower too — up to **1,000,000 won**, per the same Incheon civil-service page.

One more version of the 6-month rule matters if you're an Overseas Korean reader: if you hold Korean real estate as a Korean national and then lose that nationality — by naturalizing elsewhere, for instance — and want to keep the property, you have 6 months from the date your nationality changed to file a continued-holding report (계속보유신고), with the same up-to-1,000,000-won fine for skipping it.

## Some land needs permission before you even sign

A separate rule under Article 9 covers specific restricted categories: areas tied to military bases and national defense, designated cultural heritage sites and their protective zones, ecological and landscape conservation areas, and wildlife special protection zones. Buy land inside one of these, and you need a **land acquisition permit before you sign the contract**, not a report after.

[The gov.kr civil-service listing for this permit](https://www.gov.kr/mw/AA020InfoCappView.do?CappBizCD=15000000043) sets processing at **15 days** for an ordinary application, extendable to **30 days** for the categories listed under Article 9, paragraph 1, item 1, with a further 30-day extension possible if the review needs it. You can apply online, in person, by fax, or by mail, and the listing states the service itself is free.

## The one that actually matters if you're buying a home in Seoul or Gyeonggi

This is the rule nobody explains, because it's new. Under [the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport's own August 2025 announcement](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148947982), all of Seoul, 7 (now 9) districts of Incheon, and most of Gyeonggi Province were designated a **land transaction permit zone** for housing — single-family homes, multi-unit houses, apartments, row houses, and multi-household houses all count. Buy any of these inside the zone, as a foreigner, and you need the permit before signing, exactly like the restricted-zone rule above, but the conditions attached are much stricter:

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Move into the unit within 4 months of your permit being approved</li>
  <li>Actually live there for 2 years after acquisition</li>
  <li>Disclose where your purchase funds came from, including overseas sources, and your visa type</li>
</ul>

Break the residency condition and [the same announcement](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148947982) spells out the consequence: the district office issues a compliance order with up to a 3-month deadline, and if you still don't comply, it imposes an enforcement fine (이행강제금) — repeatedly, until you do — of up to **10%** of the property's acquisition value each time.

This zone isn't a one-year experiment that quietly expired. [The Ministry's renewal announcement](https://www.korea.kr/news/customizedNewsView.do?newsId=148970419) confirms the designation was extended for another year, through **August 25, 2027**, covering the same areas. The same renewal cites a real effect: foreign housing transactions across the Seoul metro area fell **27%** during the permit period (4,397 cases, down from 6,024 in the same months a year earlier), with Gangnam's three districts and Yongsan down **49%**, and Seocho district alone down **73%**.

## If you're not living in Korea yet: a separate report to your bank

Everything above runs under the Real Estate Transaction Reporting Act and applies to any foreigner, resident or not. If you're a non-resident buying from abroad, Korea's Foreign Exchange Transactions Act layers on a second, separate report — to a foreign-exchange bank or to the Bank of Korea, depending on where your purchase funds originate — on top of the 60-day acquisition report, not instead of it. Sources disagree on the exact fine for skipping this one, so rather than quote a number I can't confirm on a government page, ask the foreign-exchange department of whichever bank you're wiring the purchase funds through, or the Bank of Korea directly, before you transfer anything.

## When it goes wrong

**You missed the 60-day acquisition report.** File it anyway. The fine structure above (up to 3,000,000 won for a contract-based acquisition, up to 1,000,000 won for the other categories) applies whether you're a few days late or found the rule by accident months in — filing late and paying the fine is still better than an unreported acquisition sitting on the books indefinitely.

**You bought in a Seoul/Gyeonggi permit zone and can't move in within 4 months.** Contact the district office that issued your permit before the deadline passes, not after. The announcement doesn't describe a general hardship exception, so don't assume one exists — ask directly whether your specific circumstances (a job posting delay, a renovation, anything else) change the clock, rather than letting the deadline lapse and finding out only after a compliance order arrives.

**You already got a compliance order over the residency requirement.** You have up to 3 months on the order itself to fix the problem — move in, or otherwise come into compliance with whatever the order specifies. Miss that window and the enforcement fine starts, and keeps being imposed until you comply, not as a one-time penalty.

**You're not sure which of the three rulebooks applies to your purchase.** Call the city, county, or district office where the property sits before you sign anything. Which rule applies depends on the property's exact location and type, not on your visa or nationality, and a wrong guess here is the kind of mistake that's much cheaper to fix before a contract than after one.

## Checklist

<ul class="checklist">
  <li>Confirmed whether the property falls inside a restricted zone (Article 9) or the Seoul/Incheon/Gyeonggi housing permit zone</li>
  <li>If a permit zone: applied for the land/housing transaction permit before signing, not after</li>
  <li>If buying housing in the permit zone: planned to move in within 4 months and stay 2 years</li>
  <li>Filed the acquisition report within 60 days of the contract (or 6 months for inheritance, auction, or merger)</li>
  <li>If not yet resident in Korea: confirmed the extra foreign-exchange report with your bank or the Bank of Korea</li>
  <li>Kept the contract, the report receipt, and (if applicable) the permit certificate together</li>
</ul>
