Google Maps barely works here. Half the menus are in Korean. And on your first day, someone will ask for your KakaoTalk ID before your name.

I'm Jun, a Seoul local. Over the years I've helped a lot of foreign coworkers and friends settle in, and the first thing we always do is the same: sit down with their phone and install the right apps. Here are the 10 I put on every phone, what each one is actually for, and the order to set them up so you don't get stuck.

## Before you start: get a Korean phone number

Many Korean apps verify you with a text message to a Korean number. Without one, sign-ups stall. If you can, get a local SIM or eSIM first — my [guide to getting a Korean phone number](/korean-phone-number-foreigners-prepaid-postpaid/) covers prepaid vs postpaid and what each actually costs. It makes everything below much easier.

On day one, a prepaid SIM is the fastest fix: KT's own [prepaid price page](https://shop.kt.com/m/smart/phonePricePopView.do?prodCtgCd=11&usimType=LTE) lists a 10,000 won top-up that stays active for 60 days, with no sign-up fee and no contract. Once your ARC arrives and you switch carriers or plans, KT's [number-portability guide](https://help.kt.com/serviceinfo/ServiceJoinGuideL2.do) confirms the move itself costs a flat 800 won (plus VAT) on top of a 0 won sign-up fee — but you can't transfer your number again for 3 months after that.

## The ten apps

### 1. KakaoTalk

Korea's default messenger. Landlords, coworkers, delivery drivers and even government offices use it. If you don't have it, you're invisible. It's also how you'll find expat open chat rooms for your neighborhood, hobby or country.

### 2. Naver Map

The most accurate map in Korea, with real-time bus and subway arrivals and walking directions that actually work. It has an English mode. Save your home, office and favorite spots with a star so you get directions in two taps.

### 3. KakaoMap

A strong second map. Handy for checking a place's reviews and opening hours when Naver feels cluttered.

### 4. Kakao T

Korea's main taxi app. Set your pickup and destination, and you never have to pronounce an address to a driver. You can pay in the app once a card is registered, or simply pay the driver at the end of the ride.

Knowing the fare helps you catch a problem before you get in. Seoul's own city newsroom confirms the [current medium-taxi fare](https://mediahub.seoul.go.kr/archives/2006817): a base fare of 4,800 KRW covers the first 1.6 km, then the meter adds 100 KRW roughly every 131 meters. Late at night a 20% surcharge pushes the base fare to 5,800 KRW, and a steeper surcharge after that raises it to 6,700 KRW. If the fare the app shows looks far higher than that, double-check the destination pin before you confirm.

### 5. Papago

Naver's translator. It handles Korean better than most, and its camera mode reads menus and signs instantly. Use conversation mode at the pharmacy or phone shop; each person speaks their own language and it translates both ways.

### 6. Coupang

Korea's answer to Amazon. Many items arrive the next morning. Expect to need Korean phone verification to sign up.

### 7. Coupang Eats or Baemin

The two big food delivery apps. Papago's camera helps with Korean-only menus.

### 9. Daangn (Karrot)

A neighborhood second-hand market. Perfect for furnishing your first apartment cheaply. Deals happen face to face, usually near a subway exit. Check the seller's manner score before you meet.

### 10. Your bank's app

Once you've [opened a Korean bank account](/open-bank-account-korea-foreigner/), almost everything, from rent to splitting bills, happens in your bank's app.

## The setup order I recommend

1. Korean number (SIM or eSIM).
2. KakaoTalk.
3. Naver Map and Papago.
4. Kakao T.
5. After you have a bank account: Coupang, a delivery app and your bank's app.

**Jun's tip:** Some apps ask for Korean phone verification. Get a local number first and everything else gets easier.

## If an app doesn't cooperate

Most of what goes wrong with these apps has a quick fix.

**The verification code never arrives.** What if the text with your KakaoTalk or banking-app code never shows up? Wait a few minutes first, since carrier texts can lag, then request a fresh code instead of retrying the old one.

**Your sign-up is rejected on a passport-only number.** Some banking and delivery apps block sign-up outright if your line is still a prepaid passport registration rather than one tied to your ARC. Once you move to a postpaid or MVNO plan under your ARC, most of these rejections clear up on their own.

**You forget to switch your number before your ARC arrives.** If you forget to plan ahead, you can end up stuck paying for another expensive top-up. Buy the smallest prepaid top-up KT offers and switch the moment your ARC is in hand instead.

**You lose your phone with every app on it.** Call your carrier's loss line immediately. KT's own [customer center page](https://help.kt.com/store/KtCustCenter.do) confirms its foreigner line — 080-448-0100 (free) or 02-2190-1180 (paid) — takes lost-phone and suspension reports 24 hours a day, even outside its normal 09:00–18:00 hours.
